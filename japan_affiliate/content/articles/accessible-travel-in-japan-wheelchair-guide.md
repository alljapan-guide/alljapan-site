Japan has a reputation for being easy to get around, and for many travellers with a disability or a mobility need that reputation is deserved. It is not true everywhere, and the weak spots are specific: taxis, older sites and some rural areas. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes two pages on accessible travel, and this guide turns them into a planning checklist.

Where we add advice, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial opinion stay separate. Accessibility information changes, and the right check is always the operator or venue you plan to use.

## FAQ

### Are Japanese train stations accessible?

JNTO says about 95 percent of train stations are entirely accessible, and that station staff can help you get on and off the train.

### Are taxis in Japan wheelchair accessible?

Not always. JNTO says most standard cabs cannot support electric wheelchairs, so plan transfers ahead and ask Accessible Japan for advice.

### Can I rent a wheelchair in Japan?

JNTO says some tourist spots offer wheelchair rental services. Ask the venue or hotel before you go.

### Are the sidewalks easy to use?

JNTO says sidewalks are smooth, and raised yellow tiles mark safe walking paths for people with a visual impairment.

### Where do I check current details?

JNTO advises independent research for the most up-to-date information, and the operator or venue is the best source.
