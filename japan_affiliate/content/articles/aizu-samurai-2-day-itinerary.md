Aizu is the part of Tohoku where the samurai story is still told on the streets. It sits inland in Fukushima Prefecture, a few hours from Tokyo, and has a famous castle, a samurai mansion and a post town with thatched roofs. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Exploring Aizu Samurai Spirit" and adds practical notes. ## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have one day, choose the castle, the mansion and Sazaedo, and take the bus back to Koriyama in the evening. If you want a slower trip, add a night in Kitakata and plan a morning for the storehouses and ramen. If you travel in winter, check bus services and road conditions, since this is a snowy region.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Aizu suits travellers who like castles, samurai history and small towns, and who are comfortable with local buses. It is less suited to a first trip with only a few days, since the journey is long. It is a good second-week addition to a Tokyo trip.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Aizu?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### How do you get from Tokyo to Aizu?

JNTO says it takes 3 hours: the Tohoku Shinkansen to Koriyama Station takes about 1 hour 30 minutes, then a Shin-Joban Kotsu bus takes 1 hour 15 minutes.

### Why is Tsurugajo Castle famous?

JNTO says it is where one of the fiercest battles of the Boshin War of 1868 was fought, and it has a red-tiled roof that is unusual for castles in Japan.

### What is Ouchi-juku?

JNTO says it has thatched roofs and a broad main thoroughfare and was formerly one of the post towns that dotted Japan.

### What is Kitakata known for?

JNTO says Kitakata has around 4,000 traditional storehouses and over 100 ramen noodle joints.
