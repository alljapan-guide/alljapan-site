Amanohashidate is the scenic escape from Kyoto's temples: a long pine-covered sandbar across a bay on the northern coast of the prefecture. This guide follows the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) itinerary "Amanohashidate—A Scenic Seaside Itinerary". Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to pace the two days

Our view: arrive as early as the timetable allows, so the first afternoon is for the sandbar and the shrines near the station, which are within walking distance according to JNTO. Keep the second morning for the boat and the park, then return to Kyoto in the afternoon. Pack light, since the plan involves walking, a boat and a cable car, and bring layers for the sea wind.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Amanohashidate suits travellers who have already seen Kyoto's main temples and want a quieter, coastal change. It is a good choice for walkers and photographers, and for anyone who enjoys a long scenic train ride. It is a weaker fit for a short Kyoto stay, since the journey is long, or for visitors who want nightlife or shopping. ## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Kyoto to Amanohashidate?

JNTO says the Hashidate Limited Express from Kyoto Station takes about 2 hours, and the sandbar is a 5-minute walk from the station.

### How many days do I need?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### What is Amanohashidate?

JNTO describes it as a pine-covered sandbar along Miyazu Bay, considered one of the three most scenic spots in Japan.

### How do I get to Kasamatsu Park?

JNTO says to take the Amanohashidate Sightseeing Boat from the southern end of the sandbar to the northern pier, then the cable car or lift.

### Is there anything else nearby?

JNTO says Miyazu is a five-minute ride from Amanohashidate Station, with Shimasaki Park for a picnic by the bay.
