If you only have one free day, choose by travel time and interest. Kamakura is the shortest trip and has a famous Great Buddha, Hakone is built around an easy rail route and a pass system, and Nikko is the longest but is described by the tourism organisation as an outing for one or two days.

This guide compares the three using the Japan National Tourism Organization's own pages for each place. Every time and route below comes from those pages, linked in the Sources box. Where we give an opinion, we label it as our view.

## Kamakura: the shortest trip

The tourism organisation's itinerary page says that {{fact:kamakura_route}}. From Kita-Kamakura Station, it notes that Kamakura is just one train stop away, and it lists the Great Buddha (Daibutsu) of Kotoku-in Temple among the most popular destinations.

On the Great Buddha itself, the official spot page says {{fact:kamakura_daibutsu}}. It adds that you can pay a small donation and explore inside the statue, and gives the route by Enoshima Electric Railway to Hase Station followed by a 7-minute walk.

Our view: Kamakura is the easiest first day trip because the journey is short and the main sights are close together, so you can fit a temple visit and a walk by the sea into one day. The itinerary page also mentions Yuigahama Beach and traditional vegetarian Buddhist cuisine called shojin-ryori.

## Practical advice

- **Reserve where required.** The tourism organisation notes that reservations are necessary on the Nikko limited express service from Shinjuku.
- **Check the operator's page for your date.** Timetables and fares are set by the operators and can change.
- **Plan luggage.** Travel light on a day trip. If you are moving hotels, consider leaving your bags at your Tokyo hotel.
- **Pick one place per day.** Our view is that trying to combine two of these in one day leaves little time to enjoy either.

## FAQ

### Which day trip from Tokyo is the easiest?

Our view is Kamakura. The tourism organisation says it is about one hour from Tokyo by the JR Yokosuka Line to Kita-Kamakura Station, which takes 55 minutes.

### How do I get to Hakone from Tokyo?

The tourism organisation lists the direct Odakyu Railway Romance Car from Shinjuku Station to Hakone-Yumoto Station, or the Tokaido Shinkansen to Odawara Station followed by the Hakone Tozan Railway.

### Is Nikko possible as a day trip?

The tourism organisation describes the park as easy to reach for one- and two-day outings. By limited express from Shinjuku the journey takes about two hours, so it is a long day if you return the same evening.

### Do I need a pass for Hakone?

The tourism organisation says the Hakone Free Pass is sold in various combinations and will save you money. Check the pass details on the operator's page before buying.

### Can I do two of these in one day?

We do not recommend it. Each trip involves a journey of an hour or more each way, according to the official pages, so combining them leaves little time at either place.
