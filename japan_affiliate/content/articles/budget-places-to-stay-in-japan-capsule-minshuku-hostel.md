Japan has far more kinds of places to sleep than a hotel and a ryokan. ## Common mistakes

- **Booking a capsule hotel with a big suitcase.** JNTO says the space is a small cubicle, so plan for light bags.
- **Expecting a private bathroom at a minshuku.** JNTO says bathroom facilities are often shared.
- **Forgetting the bedding routine.** JNTO says you are usually expected to put away your bedding.
- **Ignoring hostel curfews.** JNTO warns that some hostels have curfews.
- **Using old prices.** JNTO's lodging pages carry older figures, so check the booking page.

## FAQ

### What is a capsule hotel?

JNTO describes capsule hotels as places that provide sleeping space in small cubicles stacked side by side, with communal bathrooms and lockers for your luggage.

### What is a minshuku?

JNTO says minshuku are often family-run, guesthouse-style lodgings, often located in rural areas, with rooms that often feature tatami flooring and futons.

### Are hostels only for young people?

JNTO says youth hostels welcome backpackers and travelers of all ages.

### Can I stay at a temple in Japan?

Yes. JNTO says many temples in Kyoto open their doors to tourists regardless of their religious affiliation, and that Koyasan is a very popular shukubo area.

### Do business hotels have private bathrooms?

JNTO says business hotel rooms are larger than capsule hotels, with private baths.
