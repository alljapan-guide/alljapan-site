The question that keeps coming up before a first trip is simple: do I need cash? The answer is yes, but not as much as it used to be, and it depends on where you go. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes two planning pages that cover this, one on cashless payments and one on credit cards. This guide follows those pages, adds a plan you can apply before you fly, and labels our own opinion as "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. For the cost of a trip itself, see our [budget travel tips](japan-budget-travel-tips.html).

## A one-week plan

For a city trip, take a card for hotels, department stores and restaurants, an IC card for trains and convenience stores, and cash for small shops. ## FAQ

### Do I need cash in Japan?

Yes. JNTO says there are still many places where cash is required, and many places in rural areas may only accept cash, even hotels.

### Which credit cards work in Japan?

JNTO says the most widely accepted cards are Visa, JCB and Mastercard, and that they are usually accepted at major hotels, department stores, shopping centers and restaurants in urban areas.

### Can I use Apple Pay or Google Pay in Japan?

JNTO says smartphone payment methods used overseas, such as ApplePay, GooglePay and AliPay, sometimes cannot be used in Japan. It names PayPay, LinePay, Rpay and MerPay as the commonly accepted apps.

### Where can I withdraw cash?

JNTO says you can withdraw cash at ATMs in Japan Post Bank and in Seven Bank.

### Can I buy an IC card with a credit card?

JNTO says that only cash can be used when purchasing from a ticket vending machine. It does not say which payment methods a station counter accepts, so ask the staff.
