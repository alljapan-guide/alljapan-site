A common worry is whether a card bought in Tokyo will work in Osaka or Kyoto, or the other way round. The official PASMO page gives a precise answer, and it is both better and more limited than people expect. This guide uses that page to explain which IC cards work together, where they do not and what to do on the lines they do not cover. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. Networks change, so check the operator before you travel.

## Common mistakes

Our view: three mistakes come up often. The first is buying a card for every region, which is not needed because the main cards work together. The second is assuming a card works on every line, which PASMO says is not the case. The third is letting the balance run low just before a long ride, so top up when you pass a machine, not at the gate. Keep the receipt from any top-up, and check the balance when you leave a region, since you may want to use up the rest before you fly home.

## FAQ

### Does Suica work in Osaka and Kyoto?

PASMO says ten transport IC cards, including Suica, PASMO and ICOCA, are interoperable nationwide, so a Suica can be used on participating lines in Osaka and Kyoto.

### Does PASMO work on every train in Japan?

PASMO says it does not mean PASMO can be used on every railway and bus line in Japan.

### Which cards are interoperable?

PASMO lists PASMO, Suica, Kitaca, TOICA, manaca, ICOCA, PiTaPa, HAYAKAKEN, nimoca and SUGOCA.

### Can I shop with an IC card?

PASMO says transit IC cards can also be used for cashless payments at participating stores.

### What if my card is not accepted?

Use cash, a paper ticket or the fare adjustment machine, and ask the station staff for help.
