Japan is one of the safest countries to visit, and it is also a country where earthquakes and typhoons are part of life. Neither should stop you from going, but a few minutes of preparation changes how calm you feel if one happens. This guide summarises what the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) tells visitors, then adds our own planning advice, labelled "Our view", so sourced guidance and editorial opinion stay separate.

## If a typhoon is coming: a simple plan

Our view: the visitors who cope best make decisions early. JNTO says that {{fact:typ_planb}}.

| Situation | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A typhoon is forecast for your travel day | Move the long trip to the day before or after | Trains and flights can be suspended |
| You are already at your hotel | Stay indoors and follow the staff | JNTO advises against going outside |
| Your train still runs | Leave early and allow extra time | JNTO says to give yourself ample time |
| You need food or water | Buy what you need, no more | JNTO says to resist panic-buying |
| The storm has passed | Use the next day for outdoor plans | JNTO says it is usually bright and sunny |

## Mistakes to avoid

Our view: these are the points where visitors lose the most time or peace of mind.

- **Leaving the hotel route unchecked.** Look at the evacuation map on the first night.
- **Booking tight connections in typhoon season.** If a flight or train is suspended, a one-hour connection becomes a lost day.
- **Heading to the coast to watch a storm.** JNTO says to avoid going outside during a typhoon, and to move to higher ground after a coastal earthquake.
- **Relying on one information source.** Save all three JNTO sources.
- **Skipping insurance.** It is the one preparation that also covers a cancelled trip.

## FAQ

### Is Japan safe for tourists?

JNTO says Japan is very safe and the crime rate is quite low, but travelers should still be aware of their surroundings and know what to do if something happens.

### What should I do during an earthquake?

JNTO says to stay calm and follow any instructions hotel or building staff give, to extinguish naked flames as quickly as possible, and to move to higher ground if you are by the coast.

### When is typhoon season in Japan?

JNTO says most typhoons form between July and October. Okinawa and Kyushu are the areas most affected.

### Will a typhoon ruin my trip?

Usually not. JNTO says typhoons tend to pass through in less than 24 hours, though your plans will likely be affected while one approaches, so have a Plan B.

### Which apps and sites should I use for alerts?

JNTO lists the Japan Safe Travel Information site, the Safety Tips app and NHK WORLD-JAPAN.
