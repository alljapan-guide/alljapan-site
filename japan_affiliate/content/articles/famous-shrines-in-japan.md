Japan has a very large number of shrines, and a first-time visitor cannot see more than a handful. This guide compares five of the best-known shrines, each covered in its own guide on this site, and suggests an order for them. It uses the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) and other official pages, and labels our own opinion "Our view", so you can tell sourced information from editorial advice.

## FAQ

### Which shrine should I visit first in Japan?

Our view is Meiji Jingu if you start in Tokyo. JNTO calls it a great destination for a first shrine visit if you have just arrived in Tokyo.

### How many shrines are there in Japan?

JNTO's etiquette article estimates around 80,000 shrines across Japan.

### Do all shrines use the same prayer?

No. The standard pattern is two bows, two claps and one more bow, but JNTO says visitors to Izumo bow twice and then clap four times before the final bow.

### In what order should I visit Ise Jingu?

JNTO says the correct sequence is the outer shrine first, then the inner one.

### Can I walk out to the torii gate at Itsukushima?

JNTO says you can walk out to the large torii gate at low tide.
