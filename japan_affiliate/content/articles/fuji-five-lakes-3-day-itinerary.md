Mt. Fuji is on almost every first-timer's list, and the Fuji Five Lakes are the easiest place to see it up close from Tokyo. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a three-day plan that visits all five lakes. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Explore the Fuji Five Lakes" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: three days is a lot for a first-time visitor who also wants Tokyo and Kyoto. If you are short of time, a one-night stay by Lake Kawaguchi still gives you the morning view, the ropeway and Oshino Hakkai. If you have more time, spread the lakes over three nights so you are not changing hotels every day.

Weather matters more here than in a city. If the mountain is hidden, switch to the caves and the indoor stops, and keep the viewpoint for the next clear morning. ## FAQ

### How many days do you need for the Fuji Five Lakes?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 3 days, but one night at Lake Kawaguchi is enough for the main views.

### How do you get to Lake Kawaguchi from Tokyo?

JNTO lists an express highway bus from the south exit bus terminal at Shinjuku, which takes approximately 1 hour 45 minutes, and a train route via Otsuki Station that takes 2 hours 5 minutes in total.

### When is the best time of day to see Mt. Fuji?

JNTO says the best views come in the morning, because haze and clouds tend to obstruct the mountain as the day wears on.

### Which lake is best for views of Mt. Fuji?

JNTO points to the north side of Lake Kawaguchi and says Lake Shoji has arguably the most gorgeous views, from its northeastern bank.

### Can you visit the caves without a guide?

JNTO says the caves have pathways and stairs, so you can enter without special equipment or guides.
