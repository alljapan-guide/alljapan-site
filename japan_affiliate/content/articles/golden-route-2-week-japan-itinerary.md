If you have two weeks for a first trip, the classic route runs from Tokyo to Osaka by way of Hakone, Kyoto, Hiroshima and Himeji. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes this as its Golden Route. This guide follows the JNTO plan day by day and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you have fewer days, drop Kurashiki and Himeji first, since they are the lightest-weight stops, then shorten Nara to a half day. With more days, add a night in Tokyo or Kyoto rather than a new city. If you travel in autumn or spring, book hotels for Kyoto early, since those seasons are the busiest.

{{warn|Do not plan every day to the hour. JNTO's plan covers many cities in 14 days, so leave a buffer for weather, delays and rest, and check opening hours for each site on the day.}}

## FAQ

### How many days does the Golden Route take?

JNTO's Golden Route is a classic two-week itinerary, with 14 days from arrival to departure.

### Which cities does it include?

JNTO says it covers Tokyo, Hakone, Kyoto, Nara, Kurashiki, Hiroshima, Himeji, Kobe and Osaka, with day trips to Yokohama and Kamakura.

### Should I use a rail pass?

JNTO recommends a 14-day JR Pass for long-distance travel, and IC cards such as Suica and ICOCA for city transport.

### How many nights in each city?

JNTO's plan is 3 nights in Tokyo, 1 in Hakone, 2 in Kyoto, 1 each in Nara, Kurashiki and Himeji, 2 in Hiroshima and 2 in Osaka.

### Is it a good first-time route?

JNTO describes it as ideal for first-time visitors, with a smooth and efficient route.
