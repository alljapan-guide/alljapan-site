Hakone is the classic escape from Tokyo: hot springs, mountain views and outdoor art, within about two hours of the city. This guide follows the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) itinerary "Hakone—An Easy Excursion From Tokyo". Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## Is Hakone a day trip or an overnight?

JNTO's itinerary is written for two days. It adds a suggestion for the stay: {{fact:hkn_ryokan}}.

Our view: Hakone can be done in one long day if you start early and travel light, but the JNTO plan has more than one day of sights. If you want a hot spring, plan the night. If you only have a day, choose the art museum or the ropeway and lake, not both.

## Who Hakone suits

Our view: Hakone suits travellers who want a change of pace from Tokyo's crowds and who enjoy a mix of art, scenery and bathing. It is a weaker fit if your priority is nightlife or shopping, or if you dislike changing between trains, buses, ropeways and boats, since the JNTO route uses several modes of transport in a single day. Allow slack for connections, and keep the day flexible.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Hakone?

JNTO says it takes 50 minutes from Tokyo Station to Hakone-Yumoto by the shinkansen and the Romance Car, or 1 hour 35 minutes from Shinjuku Station.

### How many days do I need in Hakone?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### What is the Hakone Free Pass?

JNTO describes it as a discounted pass with unlimited rides on Odakyu trains, buses, ropeways and boats, in two- and three-day versions.

### Can I see Mt. Fuji from Hakone?

JNTO says the pirate-ship cruise on Lake Ashinoko offers views of Mt. Fuji and the surrounding area.

### Is Owakudani safe for everyone?

JNTO says people with respiratory issues may want to avoid it, as sulfuric gases can be irritating on certain days.
