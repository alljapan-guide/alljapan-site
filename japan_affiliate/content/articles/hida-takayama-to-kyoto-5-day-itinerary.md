Most first itineraries run Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, but there is an alternative that moves through mountain towns and ends in Kyoto. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a five-day route from Hida Takayama through Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa to Kyoto. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Hida Takayama to Kyoto in 5 Days". Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

Our view: this route trades speed for atmosphere. It gives you old towns and villages, and it ends in Kyoto, so it can replace the usual Tokyo-first order or sit between Tokyo and Kyoto.

## Who this route suits

Our view: this route suits travellers who want to see Japan beyond the big three cities, and who are comfortable moving each day. It is a strong choice for a second trip, or for a first trip in autumn or winter when the mountain scenery is the draw. It is less suited to a short holiday, because four of the five days involve a transfer. If you have fewer days, our [Kansai 3-day itinerary](kansai-3-day-itinerary-kyoto-nara-osaka.html) is a simpler plan.

## FAQ

### How many days does the Takayama to Kyoto route take?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 5 days.

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Takayama?

JNTO says it takes 4 hours 15 minutes, with the Hokuriku Shinkansen to Nagoya and the Limited Express Wide View Hida to Takayama.

### How do I get from Takayama to Shirakawa-go?

JNTO says to take the express bus Shirakawa-go Line from Takayama Station Nohi Bus terminal to Shirakawago bus terminal.

### How do I get from Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa?

JNTO says to take the reserved Hokuriku Highway Bus from Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa Station.

### How do I get from Kanazawa to Kyoto?

JNTO says to take the JR Thunderbird Express from Kanazawa Station to Kyoto Station.
