## Matching gifts to people

Our view: a short mental list saves time at the shop. For a colleague you do not know well, a box of momiji manju is a safe choice, since it is a famous local sweet and comes in familiar flavours. For a friend who likes unusual things, the shakushi crackers or a lemon cake have a story behind them. For someone who prefers savoury food, crackers are the better pick. For a person who enjoys sake, the sake sweets are an easy way to share the region's brewing tradition without handing over a bottle. And for yourself, a small carved item from Miyajima lasts longer than anything edible. JNTO's list is broad enough that you can usually buy all of this in one stop, so choose by recipient first and by price second.

## A simple shopping plan

1. **Decide the recipients first.** Sweet lovers get momiji manju, savoury lovers get crackers, and sake fans get a sake sweet.
2. **Buy momiji manju on Miyajima** if you visit, and see the production.
3. **Choose one lasting item**, such as crackers or a carved piece.
4. **Pack the delicate items on top.** Cakes and manju are softer than crackers.
5. **Check the date.** Fresh sweets have short shelf lives, so ask the shop before you buy for a long trip.

## FAQ

### What is momiji manju?

JNTO describes it as a dessert baked in the shape of maple leaves, created in Miyajima in the early 1900s, traditionally filled with sweet red bean paste.

### Where can I buy momiji manju?

JNTO says they are popular in Miyajima, where you can see the production process, and available across the prefecture.

### What is a shakushi?

JNTO says it is a rice ladle and a good luck charm from Miyajima, and that there is an edible version called Shakushi Senbei.

### Are there souvenirs for people who do not like sweets?

JNTO lists oyster crackers and Calbee prawn crackers, which originated in Hiroshima, and Miyajima is known for wood carving.

### Is Hiroshima known for lemons?

JNTO says Hiroshima produces more than half of Japan's lemons in the Setoda area, with plenty of lemon-flavoured souvenirs.
