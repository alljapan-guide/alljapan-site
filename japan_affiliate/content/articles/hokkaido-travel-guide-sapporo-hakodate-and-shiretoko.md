Hokkaido is the biggest surprise on a map of Japan: it looks like one island of the country, but it is a large region where the distances between sights are long and the seasons change the plan completely. A summer trip means hiking and wild coasts, a winter trip means snow festivals and hot springs, and almost every route needs a flight or a long train. This page is a planning hub. It does not repeat each guide; it helps you pick a gateway, compare the JNTO routes and decide how many days to give the island. Every route comes from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), and where we add opinion we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Hokkaido?

JNTO's routes run 2 to 3 days each. Choose one route per trip, because the distances between regions are long.

### How do I get to Hokkaido from Tokyo?

JNTO says there are multiple flights per day from Haneda Airport to New Chitose Airport, and direct flights to Asahikawa and Memanbetsu for the other routes.

### Is Hokkaido good in winter?

Yes. JNTO has a dedicated 2-day winter itinerary built around Asahikawa and Sapporo.

### Can I combine Sapporo and Hakodate?

The hot spring route ends in Hakodate, and the winter route ends in Sapporo. Linking the two needs extra transfer time, so allow a full extra day.

### Do I need a car in Hokkaido?

The JNTO routes in this guide use trains, buses and flights. Some legs, such as the Shiretoko bus, are infrequent, so check timetables first.
