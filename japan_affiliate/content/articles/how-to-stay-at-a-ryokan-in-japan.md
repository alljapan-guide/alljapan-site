A night in a ryokan is one of the few travel experiences in Japan that you cannot get anywhere else, and it is also the one first-time visitors worry about most. What do you wear? Where do you eat? What if you break a rule? The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a guide to ryokan, and this page turns it into a plain-English plan: what to expect, what the etiquette really asks of you, how the pricing works and how to choose one.

Where we add advice, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial opinion stay separate. Prices and rules vary by inn, so always read the booking page of the place you choose.

## FAQ

### What is a ryokan?

JNTO describes ryokan as traditional Japanese inns found throughout the country, with tatami flooring, futon bedding and yukata robes.

### Do I have to eat dinner at the ryokan?

Usually dinner is included, and JNTO says dining in-house is an essential part of the experience. Some ryokan offer stays without meals, so check when you book.

### What do I wear at a ryokan?

A yukata, a cotton robe provided by the inn. JNTO says the staff will explain the customs, including the yukata.

### Do ryokan have onsen?

JNTO says most ryokan have an onsen, but check the inn's own page for bath types and opening hours.

### Is a ryokan good for families?

Often yes, but check bedding and room layout when you book. Our [guide to traveling with children](traveling-to-japan-with-children.html) explains what to ask about futons and cribs.
