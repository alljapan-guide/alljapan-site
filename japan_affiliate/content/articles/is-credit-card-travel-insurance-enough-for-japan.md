Many visitors assume that the credit card in their wallet already covers them, or that their health insurance at home will travel with them. Neither assumption is safe, and the official guidance explains why. This guide uses the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) page on travel insurance and turns it into a checklist you can use before you rely on a card. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. It is general information, not advice about a specific policy, so read your own documents.

## The short answer

JNTO says that {{fact:ins_recommended}}. On cards, it says that {{fact:ins_card}}. So a card may help, but JNTO treats it as something to verify, not something to assume.

Our view: treat card cover as a starting point. If you cannot answer the questions in this guide from your card's own documents, you do not yet know what you have.

Our view: the cost of a single serious treatment is the reason to check, not the likelihood. If you would not be able to pay a large bill from savings, you need cover that you have verified.

## When a separate policy makes sense

Our view: a separate policy is worth comparing if any of these apply to you.

1. You will do something active, such as hiking, skiing or cycling.
2. You have a chronic condition.
3. You cannot confirm that your card pays for treatment in Japan.
4. You are travelling with children or older relatives.
5. You would struggle to pay a large bill upfront.

## FAQ

### Is travel insurance mandatory for Japan?

JNTO says it is strongly recommended for all travelers. It does not describe it as an entry requirement.

### Does my credit card cover me in Japan?

JNTO says some credit card companies also offer travel insurance coverage, and asks you to ask your card provider for details before leaving.

### Is my home health insurance enough?

JNTO says travel insurance is a separate and different product from any health insurance you might have at home.

### Do I have to pay upfront at a clinic?

JNTO says some insurers may require you to pay upfront, and some clinics do not accept credit cards. Cashless service removes those concerns.

### Are dangerous activities covered?

JNTO says some particularly dangerous activities may not be covered, so check your policy for your planned activities.
