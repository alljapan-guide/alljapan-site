The Japan Rail Pass is worth it only when the individual fares for your own itinerary add up to more than the pass price. The only reliable way to know is to do that sum, which takes about ten minutes, and this guide shows exactly how, using the operators' own numbers.

We deliberately do not give you a blanket yes or no. A trip with many long bullet train journeys can favour the pass, while a trip that sticks to a short route may not. We show the method, the official prices, and the rules that most often catch travellers out.

## The official prices

The official Japan Rail Pass site lists the 7-day Ordinary pass for an adult at {{fact:jrp_7day_online}} when bought online. The same page lists exchange orders bought from overseas agencies at a higher price, {{fact:jrp_7day_agency}} for the same pass. The operator also publishes the 14-day and 21-day prices and the Green Car versions on that page, along with the date the current prices took effect.

{{warn|Two sellers can charge different prices for the same pass. Compare the official online price with any overseas agency before you buy, and read the exchange and refund terms.}}

Because prices and conditions have been revised before, treat any number in this article as a pointer to the official page, not as a quote. The Sources box links to the page we checked and shows the date of our last automatic check.

## How to run the numbers in ten minutes

```diagram
type: flow
caption: The break-even check
steps: List every long-distance leg you plan, with dates | Look up each one-way fare for those dates on the official fare site | Add the fares together | Compare the total with the pass price from the official page | Buy the pass only if the total is clearly higher
```

1. Write down each long leg, such as Tokyo to Kyoto or Kyoto to Hiroshima, and the date.
2. Look up the fare for each leg on the operator's fare tool. Use the reserved-seat fare if you plan to reserve.
3. Add the fares.
4. Compare the total with the pass price. If the total is lower, buy individual tickets. If it is higher, the pass saves money.
5. Remember the pass counts consecutive days from first use, not trips.

{{tip|If the two totals are close, choose the simpler option. A pass saves you buying a ticket each time; individual tickets give you flexibility on which train you take.}}

## Itineraries where the pass can win

We cannot quote fares here without a source, so think in shapes rather than numbers:

- **A big loop across several regions in one week.** Many long legs add up quickly.
- **Trips that include distant cities.** Each far destination adds a long, expensive leg.
- **Plans that change often.** Covered trains let you hop on without buying a ticket first, which is convenient, though the Nozomi rule above still applies.

## Alternatives to the nationwide pass

Regional passes cover a smaller area and often suit trips that stay in one part of the country. The Japan National Tourism Organization publishes {{fact:jnto_regional_passes}}. Check what each pass includes, what it costs and which trains it covers, because the rules differ pass by pass.

Other options worth pricing for your dates:

- **Individual bullet train tickets.** Often the simplest for a short route.
- **City subway passes.** Compare a day pass with the rides you expect.
- **Guided day trips.** If you want transport and a guide handled in one booking, tours on {{aff:klook|Klook}} can replace several train legs.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming the pass covers everything.** Check the coverage page for the lines you plan to use.
- **Forgetting about seat reservations.** Read the official page on how pass holders reserve seats.
- **Buying before your dates are fixed.** The pass counts consecutive days, so line up the start date with your first long journey.
- **Using the wrong fare for the sum.** Use the real reserved-seat fare for your actual dates from the official fare tool.
- **Trusting an old price.** Prices have been revised; use the official page.

## FAQ

### Is the Japan Rail Pass cheaper than buying individual tickets?

It depends on your itinerary. Add up the individual fares for your own legs and compare them with the price on the official page.

### Can I use the Japan Rail Pass on the Nozomi bullet train?

Not with the pass alone. The official site says a separate ticket is needed for NOZOMI and MIZUHO trains; see the page in the Sources box.

### Does the pass cover the Tokyo subway?

The official coverage page says the pass covers JR Group trains; check it for the exact lines. Most travellers also carry an IC card for city subways and private lines.

### Where should I buy the pass?

Compare the official online price with overseas agencies, and read the terms. The official price page lists both.

### Are there cheaper passes than the nationwide one?

Regional passes cover less area at a lower price. Check each operator's page for what is included.
