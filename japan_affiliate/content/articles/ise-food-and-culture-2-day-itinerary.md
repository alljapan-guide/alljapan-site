Ise is the shrine town that Japanese travellers visit for food as much as for faith. The shrine is one of the country's most important, the streets around it are full of local dishes, and the bay to the south is the home of pearl farming. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan that links them. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Food and Culture in Ise" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have a day, choose Okage Yokocho, the Inner Shrine and a long lunch, and skip the coast. If you have an extra day, add Toba and stay a night near the bay. If you visit in winter, plan for Ise lobster season.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Ise suits travellers who like food, shrines and slow walks, and who want a break from big-city sightseeing. It is a natural stop between Nagoya and Osaka. It is less suited to those who want a packed schedule, since the pace here is slow.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Ise?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### How do you get from Osaka to Ise?

JNTO says it takes 2 hours: the Kintetsu Limited Express from Tsuruhashi Station to Iseshi Station takes 1 hour 40 minutes, then a bus takes 20 minutes.

### When is Ise lobster at its best?

JNTO says Ise lobsters are at their best between October and April.

### What is tekonezushi?

JNTO says it is a signature Ise dish of delicately marinated strips of lean fish on a bed of vinegared sushi rice.

### What are Meoto Iwa?

JNTO says they are wedded rocks standing 700 meters offshore, with the rising sun seen between them from May to July.
