Many visitors do not realise that Tokyo has islands. A high-speed ferry from the city reaches Izu Oshima in under two hours, and the island has a volcano, camellia forests and an open-air hot spring by the sea. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Oshima: An Island Escape" and adds practical notes. ## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have one day, take the fastest ferry out, hike Mt. Mihara and return in the evening, but check the last sailing carefully. If you visit in January to March, add the camellia park as the main sight. If the weather is bad, the museum and the hot spring work on a wet day, so keep the hike for a clear morning.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Izu Oshima suits travellers who want an island escape that is still in Tokyo, with scenery and walking rather than shopping. It is a good second-week trip, after the main cities. It is less suited to a first visit with only a few days, because the ferry takes a large part of one of them. Pack for wind and for changeable weather, and bring a swimsuit for the bath.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Izu Oshima?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### How do you get to Izu Oshima from Tokyo?

JNTO says high-speed jet ferries depart from Takeshiba Pier to Okada Port, which takes 1 hour 45 minutes, and flights take 30 minutes.

### How long does the Mt. Mihara hike take?

JNTO says to allow one to two hours for the entire hike, including the caldera circuit.

### When do the camellias bloom?

JNTO says the camellia garden has about 1,000 species in full bloom between January and March.

### Is the Hamanoyu hot spring mixed?

JNTO says it is a mixed-gender bath, so swimsuits are necessary.
