## Who this trip suits

Our view: Izumo suits travellers who already have the classic route in hand and want something quieter and older. It is a poor fit for a rushed schedule, since the journey is long and the best parts of the visit are slow ones, such as walking the approach and sitting down for a meal. If you only have a few days in Japan, spend them on the main cities first, and keep Izumo for a second trip or for a route that already includes western Japan. If you are an enthusiast of shrines, it is one of the places that repays the effort, because the combination of mythology, architecture and the four-clap prayer makes it feel different from the shrines you will see elsewhere.

## FAQ

### Who is enshrined at Izumo Taisha?

JNTO says the shrine is said to house Okuninushi-no-Ookami, the deity of marriage.

### How do I pray at Izumo Taisha?

JNTO says visitors bow twice and then clap four times before the final bow.

### How long is the giant shimenawa?

JNTO's Izumo page gives 13.6 meters and 5.2 tons. Another JNTO article gives 13.5 metres, so treat it as approximate.

### How do I get to the shrine from the station?

JNTO says to take the Ichibata bus bound for Izumo Taisha from JR Izumoshi Station and get off at Seimon-mae.

### How long should I stay?

JNTO's itinerary recommends 2 days.
