The cherry blossom season is not a single date. It is a wave that starts in the south and moves north, so the best plan is a flexible one: pick a window, choose a base that lets you shift by a few days, and check the official forecast shortly before you travel.

This guide uses the Japan National Tourism Organization and the Tokyo tourism board as its sources, linked in the Sources box. We do not predict bloom dates, because nobody can do that reliably months ahead. Where we give advice, we label it as our view.

Our view: this is why trips that cover several regions can see more than one stage of blossoms. A route that moves north can follow the bloom, while a route that stays in one city sees one short stretch.

{{tip|If your dates are fixed, choose the region that matches them rather than the city you have always wanted to visit. A famous park out of season is less memorable than a lesser-known one in full bloom.}}

## A simple planning checklist

- **Pick a window, not a day.** The official page describes a long season, so choose a range of dates.
- **Book flexible rates.** Cancellation rules matter more than the exact price in blossom season, because plans shift with the weather.
- **Check the forecast close to travel.** Use the latest published forecast for each city on your route.
- **Plan for crowds.** Parks fill up on pleasant days; our view is that early mornings are calmer.

## FAQ

### When is the cherry blossom season in Japan?

The national tourism organisation says the season runs from the latter half of March to as late as early May, starting in the south and moving north.

### When is the best time to see cherry blossoms in Tokyo?

The Tokyo tourism board says Tokyo transforms from late March to early April. It also notes that it normally takes about one week to 10 days from flowering to full bloom.

### Can I rely on a forecast made months ahead?

No. The official forecast page states that forecasts are subject to change due to weather conditions, so check the latest one close to your travel dates.

### What if my dates are later in spring?

The national tourism organisation points to late-blooming places in Aomori and Hokkaido, and notes late spring viewing in mountain areas and Tohoku.

### Which trees will I see most often?

According to the national tourism organisation, Somei-Yoshino is the most common tree in Japan, and weeping cherry trees are often found in parks and along riverbanks.
