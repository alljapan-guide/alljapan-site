If something goes wrong in Japan, the useful thing is to have the right number before you need it. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes the key numbers for visitors, including the ones that work in English. This page collects them in one place and explains when to use each. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. Save the numbers before you travel, and in a real emergency call first and read later.

## Which number for which situation

Our view: a quick guide to choosing a number saves time when you are stressed.

- **Someone is in danger, or there is a fire or a serious medical problem:** call 119 or 110 first.
- **You are ill but it is not an emergency:** use the Japan Visitor Hotline, or ask your hotel front desk to help you find a clinic.
- **You were the victim of a theft or lost an item:** go to a koban or a police station, and get a police report if you need to claim insurance or replace a passport.
- **You need to talk to someone in English about how you feel:** use the Tokyo English Life Line.
- **You just have a question about getting around or sightseeing:** use the tourist information line, not the emergency lines.

If you are not sure, start with the hotline. It is open every hour of every day and supports several languages, so it is a safe first call when you do not know where to turn.

## FAQ

### What is the emergency number in Japan?

JNTO says it is 110 for the police and 119 for fire or ambulance.

### Is there an English helpline for the police?

JNTO says the Metropolitan Police in Tokyo has an English helpline available 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, on 03-3501-0110.

### What is the Japan Visitor Hotline?

JNTO says it is a hotline for emergency support such as illness or disasters, on 050-3816-2787, open 24 hours every day, supporting English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese.

### Where can I get mental health support in English?

JNTO lists the Tokyo English Life Line hotline on 03-5774-0992.

### What if my passport is stolen?

JNTO says to contact the issuers of stolen cards or tickets, that a police report will be required for a reissued passport, and that you will need to get in touch with your embassy.
