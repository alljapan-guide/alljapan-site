Japan is easy to enjoy as a Muslim traveller, but it takes more planning than many destinations, especially around food. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a page for Muslim travellers, and this guide summarises what it says and turns it into a planning checklist.

We are not a halal certifier, and neither is JNTO. Where we add advice, we label it "Our view", so you can tell sourced information from editorial opinion. For personal religious questions, rely on your own guidance.

## Start with one fact about certification

JNTO is clear about the system: {{fact:h_no_agency}}. That means a "halal" label can mean different things at different places, and you will need to ask questions.

The page points to several resources: {{fact:h_resources}}. Our view: use more than one, and check the date of each listing, since restaurants open, close and change menus.

## How to research a restaurant before you go

Because of the hidden ingredients above, a simple routine helps. Our view: work from the resource list to a shortlist, then confirm each place yourself.

{{photo:3|A vegetable tempura rice bowl}}

1. Find candidates in the Muslim-friendly guides that JNTO lists, near each hotel and each major sight on your route.
2. Check the most recent information on the listing, since restaurants change menus and sometimes close.
3. Message the restaurant ahead of time with your requirements, in simple wording, and ask whether alcohol is used in cooking and whether a separate kitchen is used.
4. Keep a backup plan for each day, such as snacks you trust, in case the first choice does not work out.

None of this is complicated, but it takes time. Doing it at home, before the trip, is much easier than doing it hungry in a station.

## A planning checklist

- **Before you go:** save two or three halal resources from the JNTO list, and note halal options near your hotels.
- **At restaurants:** ask about alcohol in seasonings, gelatin and animal fats, and whether a separate kitchen is used.
- **Hotels:** message ahead about prayer space and meals.
- **Daily plan:** check where you can pray near each stop.

## FAQ

### Is there a halal certification body in Japan?

JNTO says there is no central agency for halal accreditation in Japan.

### Is Japanese food automatically halal-friendly?

No. JNTO says that cooking sake, mirin and soy sauce form the basis of many seasonings, and alcohol can be found in some kinds of miso. Gelatin and animal fats may also be present in breads, snacks and sweets.

### Can I find halal groceries?

JNTO says halal-certified groceries are mostly frozen meat, spices and other single products, and that the average supermarket does not stock halal products.

### Are there prayer rooms?

JNTO says prayer rooms and mosques are mostly found in larger cities, and most major airports, tourist hotspots and Muslim-friendly hotels are increasingly offering designated spaces.

### Are there halal tours?

Yes. JNTO describes halal-friendly tours that visit famous sightseeing spots, guide you to reliable halal-friendly restaurants and schedule time for prayer.
