You do not need to carry a large suitcase through Japan's busy stations. The country has a luggage forwarding network, called Hands-Free Travel by the national tourism organisation, that lets you send bags between an airport, your hotels and your next city, so that you travel with only a day bag.

This guide uses the Japan National Tourism Organization's pages on luggage, linked in the Sources box. Prices and counters change, so we send you to the provider for current figures. Where we give an opinion, we label it as our view.

## A simple checklist

- **Ask your hotel first.** Many hotels can arrange the transfer for you.
- **Write down the address in Japanese.** Our view is that having it ready at the counter avoids mistakes.
- **Keep your receipt and tracking number.** The official page says a tracking service is available.
- **Allow extra time.** Plan for the bag to arrive after you do on some routes.
- **Check size limits.** A bag over the limit may be refused.

## FAQ

### What is Hands-Free Travel?

It is the national tourism organisation's name for Japan's luggage delivery and storage services, with certified counters marked by a logo.

### Can I send luggage from the airport to my hotel?

Yes. The official page says Narita, Haneda and Kansai International Airport all offer luggage delivery services to your hotel.

### What can I not send?

The official page lists valuables, fragile items, fresh products and liquids, and dangerous and hazardous materials.

### Do I need to book in advance?

For sending luggage from a hotel to the airport, the JNTO guide says all options require advance booking at least a day ahead.

### Is there a size limit?

The official page gives limits of 160 linear cm and a weight within 25 to 30 kg, but the exact limits depend on the carrier, so confirm with the provider.
