Using an onsen is simple once you know four things: wash thoroughly before you get in, keep your washcloth out of the water, expect full nudity, and check the tattoo policy before you go. Most of the stress for first-timers comes from not knowing these rules in advance.

This guide uses the Japan National Tourism Organization's own pages on onsen, linked in the Sources box. Where we give advice that is not from those pages, we label it as our view.

```diagram

```

The first and last steps include our own advice; the middle steps come from the tourism organisation's guide, which we quote below.

## Nudity and tattoos: check first

The tourism organisation says that {{fact:onsen_tattoo}}. It adds that some establishments are now more relaxed or offer more options for modest travellers, but that it is still best to inquire before visiting.

Our view: do this before you travel, not at the entrance. Look at the facility's website or ask your accommodation, and have an alternative in mind. The same organisation's guide on tattoos describes private baths as one option, which we cover below.

{{warn|The tattoo policy is set by each facility and can differ even between places in the same town. We cannot tell you what a particular onsen allows; ask the facility itself.}}

## Wash first, then soak

The tourism organisation explains that showers or cleansing areas are found between the changing area and the onsen, and that {{fact:onsen_wash}}. It also says to shower first and dip later.

Our view: treat the wash as the part of the visit that matters most to other bathers, because everyone shares the same water. Take your time, and rinse the area around your seat when you finish.

## Common mistakes at your first onsen

- **Asking about tattoos at the entrance.** Ask before you travel, because the policy is set by each facility.
- **Skipping the wash.** The wash comes before the bath, and the bath water is shared.
- **Letting the washcloth touch the water.** Leave it beside the bath or on your head, as the official guide describes.
- **Leaving the towel by the showers.** The guide advises you to dry off before you go back to the changing area.
- **Assuming a day visit includes a large towel.** Check what the facility provides, and bring your own if you are unsure.

## Before you go: a short checklist

- Check the facility's tattoo policy on its website or by asking.
- Decide whether you want a shared or a private bath.
- Take only what the facility allows into the bathing room; the official guide says this is just the small washcloth.
- Bring something to dry with and a change of clothes.
- Follow the signs and staff instructions at each facility, because rules can differ.

## FAQ

### Do I have to be naked in an onsen?

According to the tourism organisation, the majority of onsen require full nudity once you enter the bathing room. Small washcloths are the only thing you can bring in.

### Can I enter an onsen with a tattoo?

The tourism organisation says the majority of onsen prohibit tattooed guests, though some are more relaxed. It is best to inquire at the facility before you visit.

### Do I wash before getting in?

Yes. The tourism organisation says to shower first and dip later, sitting on a small stool and making sure no trace of soap is left.

### What do I do with the small towel?

Do not let it touch the onsen water. Leave it to the side of the bath or balance it on your head, as the tourism organisation describes.

### Is there a private option?

Yes. The tourism organisation describes private kashikiri onsen, which usually cost extra and need to be reserved, and en-suite private onsen that some accommodations include in the room price.

### Where should I try my first onsen?

Our view: choose the bath at your accommodation if you are staying overnight, because the rules and the cost are explained when you book. For a day trip, see the decision table above.

### Are footbaths a good way to start?

Yes, as a low-commitment option. JNTO mentions geothermal footbaths, called ashinoyu, which are often found near a station or the centre of a town.

### Is the bath free for overnight guests?

JNTO says a public onsen at an accommodation is almost always free of charge for overnight guests. Read your booking details to confirm what applies to you.
