{{tip|Choose gifts that are flat, sealed and wrapped. They fit into a carry-on bag and survive a trip home. If a gift is food, check the rules of your own country on bringing it in before you buy.}}

Our view: pick one category for the whole group, such as sweets for colleagues and tea for family, so you do not spend the last hour deciding. A single well-chosen item usually beats a pile of small ones.

## A simple shopping plan

1. **Decide the gifts before you reach the airport.** Make a short list of people and one gift idea each.
2. **Check the airport shops in advance.** Use the official airport website, as JNTO suggests.
3. **Leave time before security.** Several shops JNTO lists, such as the Haneda pop culture area, are before the security gate.
4. **Keep luggage in mind.** If your bag is already full, consider sending items ahead. Our [luggage forwarding guide](japan-luggage-forwarding.html) explains how.

{{warn|Do not plan to buy everything in the last hour. A late arrival at the airport, a long check-in queue or a delayed train can leave little time. Allow extra time and keep the list short.}}

## FAQ

### Can I buy souvenirs at the airport in Japan?

Yes. JNTO says the airport is an ideal place to buy souvenirs without carrying them around for the rest of your trip.

### Which airport has pop culture souvenirs?

JNTO describes Haneda's Tokyo Pop Town on the fifth floor of the passenger terminal, and Narita has a Hello Kitty store and a toy store.

### What are good gifts that are easy to pack?

JNTO lists sweets, crackers and jellies in gift-wrapped boxes, tea, small plastic figurines and Japanese stationery.

### What souvenirs are specific to Kansai?

JNTO mentions umeboshi from nearby Wakayama and treats themed on takoyaki and okonomiyaki.

### Should I check the shops before I go?

Yes. JNTO says the latest information may differ and asks you to check the official website.
