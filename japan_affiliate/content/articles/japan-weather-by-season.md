There is no single "Japan weather". The country is long, mountainous and spread across several climates, so the right time to visit depends on where you are going and what you want to do. This guide follows the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) weather page season by season, and adds practical advice that we label "Our view".

Our view: build your trip around one or two regions, not the whole country, and check the forecast for each region close to your dates.

## How to use the forecast

The JNTO page treats the forecast as something you check for each area you will visit. Our view: make a short list of the regions on your route and look at each one a few days before you leave, then again the evening before each travel day. A route that crosses several regions, such as Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka, can still see different conditions on the same day, and a single check at the start of the trip will not catch that.

Our view: pack for the season you will mostly be in, and treat the first morning as a test. If it is hot and humid, plan your outdoor sightseeing for the cooler parts of the day, and keep the afternoon for museums, department stores and long meals. If it is cool, layers are easier to adjust than one heavy coat. None of this replaces the official guidance above, but it makes the guidance easy to act on.

## FAQ

### What is the best season to visit Japan?

JNTO describes spring as a comfortable time to explore the cities and countryside, and autumn as perfect for outdoor exploration. The best choice depends on whether you want blossoms, autumn colour, snow or festivals.

### When is the rainy season?

JNTO says the rainy season begins in May to June.

### Is Japan hot in summer?

JNTO says summer throughout Japan is stiflingly hot and intensely humid, and it links heat illness prevention information.

### Are there typhoons?

JNTO says August and September may bring typhoons that can thwart your travel plans.

### Should I worry about earthquakes?

JNTO says earthquakes are a common occurrence, and it advises you to learn about earthquake preparedness and locate the evacuation area at your hotel.
