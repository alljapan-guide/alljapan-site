Ramen is a dish many first-time visitors want to try, and it is also an easy place to feel lost. The menu is short, the shop is small, and the order is often placed at a machine rather than at a table. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes a guide to ramen that explains the types, the regional styles and the etiquette. This page follows that guide, adds a practical order of operations for a visitor, and labels our own opinion as "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## FAQ

### What are the main types of ramen?

JNTO says there are four main types: miso, shio (salt), shoyu (soy sauce) and tonkotsu (pork bone stock). Stocks and seasonings are mixed from shop to shop.

### How do I order ramen in Japan?

Many ramen shops have no servers. JNTO says you insert money into a vending machine, choose your ramen, toppings, sides and drinks, and then pass the tickets to the chef.

### Is it rude to slurp ramen?

No. JNTO says slurping is not impolite and is the standard way to eat ramen. You do not need to do it perfectly.

### Should I stay long at a ramen shop?

No. JNTO says most shops are small and it is not polite to linger over your bowl, so leave soon after you finish so the next person can sit.

### What are tsukemen and mazesoba?

Tsukemen is ramen with a more concentrated soup served separately for dipping. Mazesoba is a soupless version with a strong, sauce-like seasoning.
