## How to pace the day

Our view: treat the JNTO route as a morning, an afternoon and an evening. Leave Tokyo early enough to reach Kita-Kamakura in the first half of the morning, so the temples are not rushed. Keep lunch unhurried, since a shojin-ryori meal is part of the experience rather than a quick stop. Reserve the early afternoon for the town, Komachi-dori and the Great Buddha, and leave the last hour of light for the beach. If you are short on time, drop the second Zen temple or the shopping street rather than the beach.

Check the opening hours of each temple before you go, since small sites can close earlier than a big-city sight. Our [opening hours guide](japan-opening-hours-and-holidays.html) explains the pattern.

## Who this day suits

Our view: Kamakura suits first-time visitors who want culture and a break from Tokyo without a long journey. It works for families and for solo travellers, and it works on days when the weather is uncertain, since many stops are short walks. It is a weaker choice for someone who wants nightlife, and it can be busy at weekends and in the blossom season, so go early if you can.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Kamakura?

JNTO says to take the JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station to Kita-Kamakura Station, which takes 55 minutes, and to allow about 1 hour in all.

### How many days do I need for Kamakura?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 1 day.

### How tall is the Great Buddha of Kamakura?

JNTO describes the Great Buddha of Kotoku-in Temple as a 13 meter-tall statue.

### What should I eat in Kamakura?

JNTO suggests shojin-ryori, a traditional Buddhist cuisine that is vegetarian, at restaurants in Kita-Kamakura.

### Can I visit Enoshima on the same day?

JNTO says Enoshima is a few stations away and suggests it if you have time left.
