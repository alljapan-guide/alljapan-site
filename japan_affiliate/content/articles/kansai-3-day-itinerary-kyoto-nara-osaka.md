Three days is enough to see Kyoto, Nara and Osaka without rushing, and the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has an itinerary built for exactly that. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary called "The Kansai Trio", then adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

Our view: the order works because the days get progressively easier. Kyoto is the longest walking day, Nara is mostly flat and Osaka ends with food and shopping.

## Where to stay for three days

## When to change the plan

Our view: if you have a fourth day, add it in Kyoto rather than shortening Nara. If you have only two, drop Nara or Osaka rather than compressing all three cities into a rush.

## FAQ

### How many days do I need for Kyoto, Nara and Osaka?

JNTO's Kansai Trio itinerary has a recommended length of 3 days: one for each city.

### How do I get from Kyoto to Nara?

JNTO says to take the Karasuma Line from Shijo Station to Kyoto Station, then change to the JR Miyakoji Rapid Service.

### How do I get from Nara to Osaka?

JNTO says to walk 15 minutes to Nara Station and take the JR Yamatoji Line to Osaka Castle Station.

### What should I be careful about in Nara Park?

JNTO says the deer are unafraid of people and will go through bags looking for shika senbei, so keep snacks and plastic bags out of reach.

### Can I see all of this in two days?

Our view: not comfortably. JNTO's plan gives each city a full day, so two days means dropping one city.
