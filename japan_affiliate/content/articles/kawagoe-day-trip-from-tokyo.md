## How to pace the day

Our view: start with the warehouse street while the lanes are quiet, then take the candy alley as a snack stop rather than a destination of its own. Keep the temple and the castle hall for the early afternoon, when the light is good for the old buildings and the crowds around the bell tower thin out. If you reach the town late, skip the museum and go straight to the temple, since JNTO lists the temple as one of the day's highlights.

Do not plan a tight schedule. The route is short, and the pleasure of the town is in wandering the side lanes, stopping for a sweet and reading the shopfronts. If it rains, the museum and the temple give you indoor options.

## Who this day suits

Our view: Kawagoe suits visitors who want an old-town feel without a long journey, and it works well as a first or last day trip from Tokyo. It is a good choice for families because the sweets are an easy draw. It is a weaker fit for anyone who wants a big sight or a mountain, in which case Nikko or Hakone are better. The core stops are close together, so you can shorten the day by skipping the museum or the castle hall.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Kawagoe?

JNTO says it takes 35 minutes on the TJ Liner from Ikebukuro Station to Kawagoe Station.

### How do I get from the station to the old town?

JNTO says to take a Tobu Bus to Nakamachi in 10 minutes, or walk 25 minutes.

### How many days do I need?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 1 day.

### What is Kashiya Yokocho?

JNTO describes it as a street next to the warehouse district lined with stores selling traditional sweets.

### When is the Kawagoe Festival?

JNTO says it is held on the third weekend of October.
