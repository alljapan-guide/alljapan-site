A night in a temple on a mountain is one of the most memorable things you can do in Japan, and Koyasan is the easiest place to do it. It lies south of Osaka, up a cable car, in a forest of cedars. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan for the mountain. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Spirituality in the Mountains of Koyasan" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have a day, go in the morning, see Okunoin and Kongobuji, and return in the evening, but accept that you will miss the lodging and the morning prayers. If you have more time, walk part of the pilgrimage route to the mountain, as JNTO suggests, and add a night in Osaka or Wakayama on either side. In winter, dress warmly, since the mountain is cold, and check the cable car and bus services before you leave.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Koyasan suits travellers who want a quiet, spiritual stop between busy cities, and who are happy to follow a simple routine. It is less suited to those who want nightlife or fixed menus, since temple meals are vegan and bedtime is early. It is a good choice for a second week in Japan.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Koyasan?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days, with a night in a temple lodging.

### How do you get to Koyasan from Osaka?

JNTO says it takes 3 hours: the Midosuji Subway Line to Namba Station, the Nankai Limited Express to Hashimoto Station, the local line to Gokurakubashi Station, then the Koyasan Cable Car.

### What is a shukubo?

JNTO says it is a traditional temple lodging, and there are around 52 in the area.

### What is at Okunoin?

JNTO says it is the spiritual resting place of Kukai, reached along a cedar-lined path past more than 200,000 gravestones and towers.

### What is the stone garden at Kongobuji?

JNTO says Kongobuji has the largest stone garden in the country, with two dragons created from 140 pieces of blue granite.
