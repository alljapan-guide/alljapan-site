## How to pace the three days

Our view: treat each day as a rail leg with one anchor, not a list of sights. On day 1 the anchor is the castle and the first evening in a hot spring town, on day 2 it is the baths and the stations along the coast, and on day 3 it is the coastal view and the arrival in Kagoshima. Build in time between trains, since the line has fewer services than a big-city route, and decide in advance whether you want the restaurant train, because that choice fixes your timetable. Pack a day bag and send a larger case ahead, which our [luggage forwarding guide](japan-luggage-forwarding.html) explains.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: this route suits travellers who enjoy scenic train rides, hot springs and small towns, and who have a few days to spare in Kyushu. It is a poor fit for a short trip, since the journey from Tokyo is long, or for travellers who want city nightlife. It is also easier with light luggage, because the plan involves several train changes and short walks.

## FAQ

### How many days does the itinerary take?

JNTO's Southern Kyushu by Rail itinerary has a recommended length of 3 days.

### How do I get to Kumamoto from Tokyo?

JNTO says it takes 5 hours 40 minutes by shinkansen via Hakata, or 1 hour 45 minutes by flight from Haneda.

### What is the Hisatsu Orange Railway?

JNTO describes it as a line along the west coast of Kyushu through hot-spring resorts and seaside scenery, connecting seven cities and towns.

### Is there a restaurant train?

JNTO says the Orange Shokudo restaurant train runs on Saturdays, Sundays and some holidays, with tables reservable up to two months ahead.

### What can I see in Kagoshima?

JNTO suggests Temmonkan for local food and a ferry to the Sakurajima volcano.
