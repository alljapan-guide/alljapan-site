The Kumano Kodo is the walk that people mean when they say they want to see rural Japan. It is a network of old pilgrimage routes, far from the usual rail lines, and it ends at the tallest waterfall in the country. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a short plan that links the three great shrines. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Trekking the Kumano Kodo Pilgrimage Route" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you have only two days, keep Hongu Taisha, Kawayu and Nachi, and skip the optional waterfalls. If you want a long hike instead of buses, choose one section of the old route and book accommodation at each end, since the shrines are linked by trails as well as roads. If you travel in winter or after heavy rain, check bus services first.

For a longer route that links this region with other pilgrimage sites, our [Shikoku itinerary](shikoku-6-day-itinerary.html) shows another walking-friendly region, and our [shrine etiquette guide](shinto-shrine-etiquette.html) explains how to behave at a shrine.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: the Kumano Kodo suits travellers who like walking, quiet places and slow travel, and it is a poor fit for a tight schedule. The shrines are far from the main rail lines, so the real cost of the trip is time, not distance. If you can only spare two days, treat it as a taste of the route rather than a full pilgrimage. Book your accommodation before you go, because the choices in small villages are limited, and keep the first and last bus times in your phone so that you always know your fallback if a walk takes longer than planned.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for the Kumano Kodo?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2-3 days.

### How do you get to Hongu Taisha?

JNTO says there are seven buses a day from Kii-Tanabe Station to Hongu Taisha Shrine, and the last one departs at 4:40 p.m.

### How tall is Nachi Falls?

JNTO says Nachi Falls is 133 meters tall and has the highest drop of any waterfall in Japan.

### What are the Kumano Sanzan?

JNTO says they are the three Kumano shrines: Kumano Hongu Taisha, Kumano Hayatama Taisha and Nachi Taisha.

### Can you stay overnight near the route?

JNTO says Kawayu Onsen is home to a number of ryokan for those wanting to spend the night.
