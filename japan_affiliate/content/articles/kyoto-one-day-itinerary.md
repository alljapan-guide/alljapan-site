One day is not enough for Kyoto, but it is enough for a good walk. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) published a walking itinerary for exactly that: a day on foot along the eastern hills, from a Zen temple to the geisha district. This guide follows the JNTO blog "24 Hours in Kyoto" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

One caution first: {{fact:ky1_date}}. Opening hours, museum closures and events can change, so check each stop before you go. JNTO sets the tone: {{fact:ky1_intro}}.

Our view: the order makes sense because it runs downhill and southwards, ending in the shopping and dining district. It also starts early, which JNTO recommends.

## Who this day suits

Our view: this plan suits first-time visitors who want Kyoto's temples, gardens and canals in one walk, and who are comfortable on their feet for most of a day. It is a weaker choice if you want a western Kyoto stop such as Arashiyama or a southern one such as Fushimi Inari, since the JNTO route stays in the east. If you have more time, add a second day. ## FAQ

### Can you see Kyoto in one day?

JNTO says 24 hours in Kyoto is an inadequate amount of time to see everything, but its walking itinerary covers the highlights in the east.

### What time should I start?

JNTO says early-morning starts pay off in Kyoto, especially in spring and autumn, and its plan starts at 8:30 am.

### How long is the Philosopher's Path?

JNTO says it takes roughly another 30 minutes to reach the end of the path, where the walk continues by road.

### Where do I see geisha?

JNTO says Shinbashi Street in Gion has beautifully preserved teahouses that host exclusive geisha receptions.

### How old is the JNTO blog?

It was published on Feb. 6, 2020, so check current hours and events at each stop.
