This guide follows the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) itinerary "A Cultural Journey of Kyushu". ## How to pace the three days

Our view: this is a high-transfer plan, since each day ends in a different city. Reserve seats on the shinkansen legs where you can, keep luggage light, and decide in advance whether you want a night in Nagasaki, Kumamoto or Kagoshima. If you have a fourth day, add it in Kumamoto or Kagoshima rather than Nagasaki, since the later legs are the longest.

## What to book ahead

Our view: the pieces most worth booking early are the shinkansen seats on the long legs, the hotel in Kumamoto if you travel in cherry blossom season, and any theme park or garden event you care about. The route is flexible enough to change if one booking falls through, since each city has several indoor options.

## Who this route suits

Our view: this route suits travellers who want history, a castle, gardens and a volcano in one trip, and who are comfortable on several train legs. It is a strong choice for a second trip to Japan, or for a first trip that has already covered Tokyo and Kyoto. It is less suited to a very short stay, because the travel time between cities uses a large part of each day.

## FAQ

### How many days does the itinerary take?

JNTO's A Cultural Journey of Kyushu has a recommended length of 3 days.

### How do I get from Tokyo to Nagasaki?

JNTO says it takes 6 hours 35 minutes by shinkansen via Hakata and a limited express, or you can fly from Haneda to Nagasaki Airport.

### What is Huis Ten Bosch?

JNTO describes it as a theme park that recreates old Europe with Dutch-style buildings and canals, with a Tulip Festival in spring and a Kingdom of Lights in winter.

### What is Sengan-en?

JNTO describes it as a former villa and garden, collectively a UNESCO World Heritage site.

### Can I see Kumamoto Castle after the earthquake?

JNTO says the castle was damaged in the 2016 earthquake and remains a stately symbol of the city, surrounded by over 800 cherry blossom trees.
