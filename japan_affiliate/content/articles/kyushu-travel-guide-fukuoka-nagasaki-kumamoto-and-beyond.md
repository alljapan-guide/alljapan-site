Kyushu is the third big island of Japan's usual travel map, and it rewards a second trip better than a first. It has castles, hot springs, volcanoes and a long port history, but it is a region where the question is not what to see; it is how to link the places without losing days on trains. This page is a planning hub for the island. It points to our detailed guides, compares the routes in one place, and helps you choose where to enter and where to end. Every route comes from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), and where we add opinion we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

Our view: for most visitors the best plan is to enter through Fukuoka and leave from a different city, such as Kumamoto or Kagoshima, rather than loop back. A one-way route saves a full day, which matters on an island where the sights are far apart.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Kyushu?

JNTO's Kyushu routes run 3 to 4 days each. A first trip works well with one 3-day route, plus a day in Fukuoka at the start.

### What is the best way to get to Kyushu?

JNTO says it takes five hours by shinkansen from Tokyo to Fukuoka, so many visitors fly instead. The shinkansen from Shin-Osaka to Hakata Station takes around two hours and 20 minutes.

### Is Fukuoka a good base for Kyushu?

Yes for the north and west. JNTO says the airport is six minutes from Hakata Station by subway, which makes the first and last day easy.

### Can I do hot springs without a car?

Yes. The Kumamoto to Kagoshima plan is built around trains, though some hot springs need a short taxi or walk from the station.

### Is Okinawa part of Kyushu?

No. Okinawa is a separate island group. Our guide covers it as its own 3-day trip.
