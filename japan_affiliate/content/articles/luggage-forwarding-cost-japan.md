The price of sending a suitcase in Japan is one of the most searched questions about luggage, and the honest answer is a range that depends on the carrier, the route and the size. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes sample figures, and it is clear about what they are. This guide collects those numbers, explains what changes them and shows how to get a quote. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## A worked way to compare

Our view: compare three numbers for your own trip, and write them down before you decide. First, the delivery quote for your route and size. Second, the cost of the alternative, such as a taxi, a pre-booked transfer or a train with the bag. Third, the value of your time and energy on arrival day. A fee that looks high next to a train ticket can look small next to an hour spent on crowded platforms with a heavy bag. If you send more than one bag, ask whether a second bag is priced the same, since a family can pay more than one traveller expects.

## Is it worth the cost?

Our view: think about what the fee buys. It saves you from carrying a heavy suitcase across stations at rush hour, and from the stairs and crowds that JNTO mentions. It also lets you start sightseeing earlier on arrival day. If your journey is short, or you are travelling light, a locker or a taxi may cost less.
