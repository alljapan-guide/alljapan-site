Before you hand a suitcase to a counter in Japan, it helps to know what the service will and will not take. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) lists limits on size, weight and value, and a short list of items that cannot be accepted. This guide turns that list into a pre-flight check. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. Rules differ by carrier, so confirm with the one you use.

Our view: pack as if the bag will be handled by several people. Soft items such as clothes are the safest contents. Liquids and fresh food are on the banned list, so do not buy a local food gift and put it in a bag to be sent.

## A packing checklist

1. **Dimensions.** Add width, height and depth, and compare with the limit above.
2. **Weight.** Compare with the weight range in JNTO's note.
3. **Contents.** Remove anything on the refused list.
4. **Labels.** Put your name, your hotel and your phone number inside and outside.
5. **Documents.** Carry your passport, tickets and medicine yourself.
6. **Receipt.** Photograph the form and the receipt.

{{tip|Pack a small day bag with a charger, medicine, a change of clothes and your documents. If the main bag arrives late, you will still have what you need for the first evening.}}

## FAQ

### What are the size and weight limits for luggage forwarding in Japan?

JNTO says that {{fact:hf_limits}}.

### What cannot be sent?

JNTO says valuables, fragile items, fresh products and liquids, and dangerous and hazardous materials cannot be accepted.

### Is there compensation for a lost bag?

JNTO says compensation for damaged or lost items is provided, and the amount may vary depending on the carrier.

### Can I track my luggage?

JNTO says a shipment tracking service is available.

### Can I send a snowboard or a bicycle?

JNTO's guide says most companies also offer delivery for odd-sized luggage like sports gear, but ask your carrier for the size rules and the price.
