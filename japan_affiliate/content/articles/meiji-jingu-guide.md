## A simple plan for a morning visit

Our view: this order works well for a first visit, using only what the JNTO page describes.

1. **Arrive by train** at Harajuku Station or Meiji-jingumae, and walk about 10 minutes into the forest.
2. **Walk slowly to the main sanctuary** and notice how quickly the city noise fades under the trees.
3. **Do the shrine routine** at the main sanctuary, using the English signage JNTO mentions and our [etiquette guide](shinto-shrine-etiquette.html).
4. **Choose one extra stop**: the Inner Garden, the museum, or a wish on an ema tablet.
5. **Finish in Harajuku or Yoyogi Park** for lunch or a walk.

Allow a little more time than you think you need, since the walks through the grounds are part of the visit rather than the gaps between sights.

## FAQ

### How do I get to Meiji Jingu?

JNTO says to take the Yamanote Line to Harajuku Station, or the Chiyoda or Fukutoshin lines to Meiji-jingumae. The shrine is a 10-minute walk from either.

### Who is Meiji Jingu dedicated to?

JNTO says it is dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken.

### When was the shrine built?

JNTO says it was built in 1920, and that the buildings destroyed during World War II were rebuilt after a public fundraising effort.

### Is there an entrance fee?

JNTO says the Inner Garden requires an entrance fee. Check the current details on the official pages.

### When is the busiest time?

JNTO says nearly three million visitors swarm the area on New Year's Day, and there is a Spring Grand Festival from late April to early May.
