Android users often ask whether they can do what iPhone users do: put a transit card on the phone and tap through the gate. The official PASMO site answers that for phones bought outside Japan, and the answer is a clear caution. This guide uses the PASMO page, explains what it means for a visitor and lists the practical alternatives. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. Support changes, so check the PASMO site before you travel.

## A short checklist for Android users

Our view: a few checks make the first day easier.

1. Look at your phone's settings and note where and when it was bought, since PASMO's note depends on that.
2. Read the PASMO page for the latest notes on Mobile PASMO, and do not rely on forum posts that may be out of date.
3. Plan your first train ride with a paper ticket, and keep the ticket until you leave the station, since you need it at the exit gate.
4. Keep some cash for buses and small shops.
5. If you travel with someone who has an iPhone, they may be able to use a card on their phone, but each traveller needs their own way to pay.

## FAQ

### Can I use Mobile PASMO on an Android phone bought outside Japan?

PASMO says that, in general, Android devices purchased outside of Japan are not compatible with Mobile PASMO.

### Is Mobile PASMO available in English?

PASMO says support for Mobile PASMO is only available in Japanese.

### What can I use instead?

You can use paper tickets, a physical IC card if one is on sale, or a card on an iPhone if you have one.

### Does PASMO work on every train in Japan?

PASMO says it is not accepted on all train and bus networks in Japan.

### Is there a card for international visitors?

PASMO's notice of April 17, 2026 said that TOURIST PASMO, an IC card for international visitors, would be available soon. Check its current status.
