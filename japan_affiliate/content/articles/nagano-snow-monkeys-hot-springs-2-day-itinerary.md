Wild monkeys bathing in a hot spring is one of the images people associate with Japan, and it is closer to Tokyo than many visitors expect. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan that links the monkeys with hot spring towns in northern Nagano. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Snow Monkeys and Hot Springs in Nagano" and adds practical notes. ## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have one night, skip Takayama Onsen Village. Take the first train to Nagano, go to the monkeys, stay in Shibu Onsen and return the next morning. If you travel in deep winter, give yourself extra time for each connection and check the weather before you leave, since snow can slow buses and trains. If you travel with children or with limited mobility, the walk to the park and the stairs in hot spring towns are worth checking in advance.

## FAQ

### How long should you spend on a Nagano snow monkey trip?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days, and the monkey park and one hot spring stay fit comfortably into a single night.

### How do you get from Tokyo to the snow monkeys?

JNTO says to take the Hokuriku Shinkansen from Tokyo to Nagano Station in 1 hour 45 minutes, then a Nagano Dentetsu train to Yudanaka Station and a bus to the Snow Monkey Park stop.

### Can you see the snow monkeys outside winter?

JNTO says you can enjoy watching the monkeys all year round, and especially in winter, when they bathe in the hot springs.

### Where should you stay in the area?

JNTO recommends an overnight stay in Shibu Onsen, a hot spring resort on the banks of the Yokoyu river, with footbaths and day-use hot springs if you are short of time.

### Is there skiing nearby?

JNTO says northern Nagano receives a lot of snow in winter and has numerous ski resorts, including Yamaboku Wild Snow Park and the 18 interconnected resorts of Shiga Kogen.
