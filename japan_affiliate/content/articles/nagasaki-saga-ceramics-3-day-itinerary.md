Nagasaki is the Japanese city that feels least like the others. It was the port that stayed open to the world, and it still shows Chinese, European and Japanese influence on the same few streets. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) pairs the city with the pottery towns of neighbouring Saga. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Nagasaki and Saga: Diversity and Ceramic Heritage" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have two days, keep Nagasaki for the first day and choose Arita or Karatsu for the second, not both. If you are interested in pottery, choose Arita and Okawachiyama; if you prefer a seaside town with a castle, choose Karatsu. Because the trains between Nagasaki and the pottery towns involve a change, check timetables before you go, and plan for a late return if you add a meal.

If you travel with children or with limited mobility, the hills around Oura Cathedral and Glover Garden are the part to check in advance. ## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Nagasaki and the pottery towns?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 3 days, with one for Nagasaki and one each for Arita and Karatsu.

### How do you get from Tokyo to Nagasaki?

JNTO says to take the JR Tokaido-Sanyo Shinkansen to Hakata Station, about five hours, then change for Nagasaki, or fly from Haneda Airport to Nagasaki Airport, which takes about two hours.

### What is Arita known for?

JNTO says Arita in Saga Prefecture is the origin of Japanese porcelain, with an almost transparent white base and vividly drawn pictures.

### When is the Arita Ceramics Fair?

JNTO says it is held every year during Golden Week, from April 29 to May 5.

### What should you eat in Nagasaki?

JNTO points to Nagasaki's Chinese-influenced dishes, including the chanpon noodle dish, which you can try in Chinatown.
