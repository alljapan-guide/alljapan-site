Your first hour in Japan sets the tone for the trip, and the way from the airport to your hotel is the part most visitors worry about. Tokyo has two airports, and the best route depends on which one you land at, how much luggage you carry and what time you arrive. This guide uses the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) airport pages for Narita and Haneda, and turns them into a simple choice. Where we add opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. Timetables and fares change, so check the operator's page for the day you travel.

## Choosing between train, bus and taxi

| Your situation | Best choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Landing at Narita, hotel near a big west-side station | Narita Express | JNTO lists the main urban stations on its route |
| Landing at Narita, hotel in the north of Tokyo | Keisei Skyliner | JNTO says it is convenient for the north |
| Landing at Haneda, hotel near Shinagawa | Keikyu Line | JNTO says it runs directly from all three terminals |
| Landing at Haneda, hotel near Hamamatsucho | Tokyo Monorail | JNTO says it connects all three terminals there |
| A lot of luggage | Limousine bus | JNTO calls it a good option with a lot of luggage |
| A group of travellers | Taxi | JNTO says it can be a reasonable choice for a group |
| Arriving very late | Check last trains first | Last services differ by airport and mode |

Our view: the table is a starting point, not a rule. If you are tired after a long flight, the route with the fewest changes is usually the best one, even if another route is a few minutes faster.

## Common mistakes on arrival day

- **Choosing a route without checking the last train.** A delay can turn an easy plan into a hard one.
- **Buying the wrong ticket for a rail pass.** JNTO says that the Skyliner is not covered by a Japan Rail Pass, while the Narita Express is covered. Our [rail pass guide](is-japan-rail-pass-worth-it.html) explains how to decide whether a pass pays off.
- **Going to the wrong terminal.** Narita and Haneda both have several terminals, so check the terminal on your ticket before you pick a route.
- **Carrying a large bag through a station change.** If your route needs two changes, a bus or a taxi may be easier.
- **Arriving with no data.** Set up an eSIM or collect a SIM or pocket WiFi at the airport, so you can read a map when you leave.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Narita Airport to central Tokyo?

JNTO says the Narita Express gets you to Tokyo Station in just under an hour, and the Keisei Skyliner reaches Nippori and Ueno in around 40 minutes.

### Is Haneda closer to Tokyo than Narita?

Yes. JNTO says Haneda is the closest airport to Tokyo, around 30 minutes by train from the center of Tokyo, while Narita is about 60 kilometers east of central Tokyo.

### Can I use a Japan Rail Pass on the airport trains?

On the Narita Express, yes: JNTO says that if you hold a Japan Rail Pass, your Narita Express ticket is issued free of charge. On the Keisei Skyliner, no: JNTO says Japan Rail Passes cannot be used.

### What if my flight lands after the last train?

JNTO gives the last services for each airport, and notes that you can stay at an airport hotel, such as the capsule hotel connected to Narita Terminal 2 or the Royal Park Hotel at Haneda Terminal 3. A bus or a taxi may also run later.

### Is a taxi a good choice from the airport?

JNTO says flat-rate taxi fares are available to most central destinations, and that a taxi is more expensive than other options but can be a reasonable choice for a group.
