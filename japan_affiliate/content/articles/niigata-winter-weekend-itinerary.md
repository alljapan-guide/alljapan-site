Niigata is the place to go when you want real snow without a long journey. It lies on the Japan Sea side of Honshu, one bullet-train ride from Tokyo, and its winter is serious. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a two-day plan that mixes skiing, hot springs and local craft. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "A Winter Weekend in Niigata" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have one day, choose Echigo-Yuzawa for skiing and skip Shiozawa. If you are travelling in a group with mixed interests, send the skiers to the slopes and take the others to the hot springs and the museum, then meet for dinner. If you do not want to drive, stay near the station so that the evening is easy to organise.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: this weekend suits travellers who want a snow experience without leaving the Tokyo area for more than a night. It suits families with beginner skiers as well as experienced skiers, because the resort has courses for all levels. It is less suited to travellers who dislike cold weather, and you should pack for it with warm layers, waterproof boots and gloves, because the walk between a station, a bus stop and a hot spring can be longer than it looks on a map.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for a Niigata winter trip?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### How do you get from Tokyo to Muikamachi?

JNTO says to take the JR Joetsu Shinkansen from Tokyo Station to Echigo Yuzawa, then the local JR Joetsu Line to Muikamachi Station, two stops away.

### How do you get to Hakkaisan Ski Resort?

JNTO says it is a 20-minute drive or taxi ride from Muikamachi Station, and there are infrequent buses.

### Can beginners ski at Hakkaisan?

JNTO says the 11-course resort has options for all levels, and English-language lessons are available.

### What is Shiozawa known for?

JNTO says it is known for the 1,200-year-old craft of making plain-woven hemp textiles.
