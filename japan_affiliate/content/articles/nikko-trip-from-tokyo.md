## One day or two?

JNTO's national parks page says that {{fact:nikko_distance}}. The itinerary itself is written for two days.

Our view: a one-day trip works if you start early and keep to the shrines and the Chuzenji viewpoints. Two days let you stay by the lake and add a hike. If you visit in autumn, JNTO says the lake is perhaps most lovely then, so book ahead for a stay.

## Who Nikko suits

Our view: Nikko suits travellers who want history and scenery in one trip, and who are comfortable with a day that mixes trains, buses and a ropeway. It is a strong choice for autumn colours and for anyone who has already seen the main Tokyo sights. It is less suited to a very short stay in Tokyo, because the journey takes a large part of a day. Plan the bus connections before you leave, and keep a buffer for the return train.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Nikko?

JNTO says about 2 hours, by the shinkansen to Utsunomiya and then the Nikko Line, or by Limited Express from Tobu Asakusa Station to Tobu Nikko Station.

### How many days do I need in Nikko?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days, and the national parks page says the park is easy to reach for one- and two-day outings.

### What is Nikko Toshogu Shrine?

JNTO says it is dedicated to Ieyasu Tokugawa, and its Yomei-mon Gate is covered in over 500 ornate carvings.

### What is Kegon-no-taki Falls?

JNTO calls it Nikko's most famous waterfall, with an elevator 100 meters down to the base.

### Can I use a pass for the buses?

JNTO says you can use the Nikko pass for the Tobu train and the Tobu bus network.
