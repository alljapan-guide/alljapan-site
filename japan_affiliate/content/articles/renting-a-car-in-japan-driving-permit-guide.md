A rental car is the easiest way to reach places that trains and buses serve badly: the Kumano Kodo trails, rural Hokkaido, the Shimanami Kaido islands or a hot spring town in the mountains. It is also the one transport choice in Japan where a missing document can end the plan at the rental counter. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a page of things to know when renting a car, and this guide turns it into a checklist.

Where we add advice, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial opinion stay separate. Rules change, so check your national automobile association and the rental company before you travel.

## FAQ

### Can I drive in Japan with my home licence?

JNTO says you must obtain an International Driving Permit before you leave, with exceptions for visitors from a few countries who carry a Japanese translation.

### Is a digital driving permit accepted?

No. JNTO says card-type and smartphone versions are not valid in Japan. A booklet-type paper permit is.

### Which side of the road do cars drive on?

Cars drive on the left side of the road, and JNTO notes that many accidents happen when cars turn.

### Do I need an ETC card?

JNTO advises getting an ETC card from the rental company if you use expressways, and settling the tolls when you return the car.

### Why does the entry stamp matter?

JNTO says your date of arrival is confirmed by the entry stamp, and that automated gates do not give one. The permit's validity is tied to that date.
