## How to pace the six days

Our view: the JNTO plan has one major transfer almost every day, so keep luggage light and book the longer bus and train legs early. If you have fewer days, keep Tokushima or Takamatsu, Kochi and Matsuyama, and drop the Miyoshi mountain day, which is the hardest to reach. If you have more, add a night in Matsuyama and a second soak at Dogo Onsen.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Shikoku suits travellers on a second or third trip who want castles, festivals and hot springs without the crowds. It is a weaker fit for a first trip with a short stay, since the transfers use a large part of each day. It is also a good choice for travellers who prefer trains and buses to driving.

## FAQ

### How many days does the itinerary take?

JNTO's Shikoku's Nature and Traditions has a recommended length of 6 days.

### How do I get to Tokushima?

JNTO says to fly from Haneda to Tokushima Airport in 1 hour 10 minutes, or take the Nankai Express Bus from Osaka in 2 hours 20 minutes.

### When is the Awa-Odori festival?

JNTO says it is a four-day dance festival held from August 12 to 15 every year.

### What is Dogo Onsen?

JNTO calls it the oldest spa in Japan, less than 20 minutes by tram from Matsuyama.

### Which castles can I see?

JNTO says Kochi Castle was built in 1753 and Matsuyama Castle in 1602, and both are among the 12 original castle keeps.
