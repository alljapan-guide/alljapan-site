## A note on being respectful

Our view: a shrine is a place of worship, so treat it as one even if you are a tourist. Keep your voice low, follow the signs about where photos are allowed, and give people who are praying a little space. You do not need to share the beliefs to take part in the steps, and JNTO frames the routine as something anyone can do once they have learned it. If you are unsure whether a gesture is appropriate, watch for a moment and ask a staff member at the shrine office.

## FAQ

### How do I pray at a Shinto shrine?

JNTO says to toss an offering into the box, ring the bell, bow twice, clap twice, make your wish and then bow once more.

### How do I purify my hands?

JNTO says to take the ladle in your right hand and pour water over your left, then switch hands, and finally hold the ladle vertical so the remaining water pours over the handle.

### Which part of the path should I walk on?

JNTO says to walk on either the left or the right of the path, as the centre is reserved for the kami.

### What are omamori?

JNTO describes them as small good luck charms you can attach to a bag or keep in a wallet, with versions for exams, health, luck, happiness and traffic safety.

### What is a goshuin?

JNTO says goshuin are red seals received from a shrine office to signify a visit, usually hand written and unique to each shrine.
