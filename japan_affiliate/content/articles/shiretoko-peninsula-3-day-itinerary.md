Shiretoko is the part of Hokkaido that most visitors never reach, and that is the point. It is a peninsula of forest, cliffs and sea in the far northeast, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a three-day plan that mixes sightseeing with outdoor activities. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "Into the Wild: The Shiretoko Peninsula" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have two days, keep day 1 and day 3 and treat the waterfalls as a quick stop. If you are travelling in winter, remember that many activities depend on the ice, the snow and the weather, so keep one day free. If you prefer comfort, base yourself in one town rather than moving each night.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: Shiretoko suits travellers who have already seen the main cities and want scenery, wildlife and quiet. It is less suited to a first week in Japan, because the journey takes most of a day each way. If you want a gentler Hokkaido trip, the hot springs route in the south is easier to reach.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Shiretoko?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 3 days.

### How do you get to Shiretoko from Tokyo?

JNTO says it takes 4 hours: a 1-hour 40-minute flight from Tokyo Haneda to Memanbetsu Airport, then the Shiretoko Airport Liner Express bus to Utoro Onsen Bus Terminal, around 2 hours.

### When does the sightseeing ship run?

JNTO says it runs from late April to about late-October.

### How long does the Five Lakes walk take?

JNTO says the walking path around all five lakes takes 60 minutes, while walking around one or two of the lakes takes just 30 minutes.

### Is Kamuiwakka Falls safe to climb?

JNTO says visitors can enjoy shower climbing in hot water, but some elementary knowledge and skill is needed because the rocks are slippery.
