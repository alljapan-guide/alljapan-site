## Why this beats a souvenir shop

Our view: the difference between a shotengai and a tourist shop is the audience. The shops are there for neighbours, so the goods are everyday items, and the souvenirs tend to be small, local and tied to the street itself. That gives you two advantages. The items are less likely to be the same as everyone else's, and you see a part of the city that a main sight does not show. The trade-off is that shops can be small, and not all of them are set up for visitors, so a few polite words and a willingness to point and smile go a long way.

## FAQ

### What is a shotengai?

JNTO says it is a covered shopping arcade or shopping street in Japan, typically reserved for pedestrians and occasionally cyclists.

### Why shop on a shotengai?

JNTO says each street is different and has souvenirs specific to it, and that it caters primarily to local residents.

### What can I buy at Nishiki Market?

JNTO suggests chili oils and furikake, which are shelf-stable and travel-friendly, and says you can sample them before you buy.

### What is a good lightweight gift from Osaka?

JNTO suggests a tenugui, a hand towel, including hand-dyed designs showing Osaka landmarks.

### What is onsen manju?

JNTO describes them as sweet buns, usually filled with bean paste, traditionally cooked in hot spring steam, and a popular Atami souvenir.
