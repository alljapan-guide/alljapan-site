## Why this beats a souvenir shop

Our view: the difference between a shotengai and a tourist shop is the audience. The shops are there for neighbours, so the goods are everyday items, and the souvenirs tend to be small, local and tied to the street itself. That gives you two advantages. The items are less likely to be the same as everyone else's, and you see a part of the city that a main sight does not show. The trade-off is that shops can be small, and not all of them are set up for visitors, so a few polite words and a willingness to point and smile go a long way.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying soft sweets for a long trip.** Our view: manju are soft and best eaten soon after you buy them.
- **Forgetting food rules at home.** Our view: check what your own country allows before you buy food, especially seasonings.
- **Arriving when the shops are shut.** Our view: the shops are set up for local customers, so check hours before you go. See our [opening hours guide](japan-opening-hours-and-holidays.html).
- **Expecting a tourist set-up.** JNTO says a shotengai usually caters primarily to local residents, so patience and a few polite words go a long way.
- **Paying without checking how.** Our view: payment options differ by shop, so read our [cash and cashless guide](cash-cards-and-cashless-payments-in-japan.html) before you go and keep some cash.
- **Buying heavy items early in a long trip.** Our view: keep bags light, and see our [luggage forwarding guide](japan-luggage-forwarding.html) if you carry a lot.

## FAQ

### What is a shotengai?

JNTO says it is a covered shopping arcade or shopping street in Japan, typically reserved for pedestrians and occasionally cyclists.

### Why shop on a shotengai?

JNTO says each street is different and has souvenirs specific to it, and that it caters primarily to local residents.

### What can I buy at Nishiki Market?

JNTO suggests chili oils and furikake, which are shelf-stable and travel-friendly, and says you can sample them before you buy.

### What is a good lightweight gift from Osaka?

JNTO suggests a tenugui, a hand towel, including hand-dyed designs showing Osaka landmarks.

### What is onsen manju?

JNTO describes them as sweet buns, usually filled with bean paste, traditionally cooked in hot spring steam, and a popular Atami souvenir.

### Which shotengai is easiest for packable gifts?

JNTO points to Nishiki Market in Kyoto for shelf-stable chili oils and furikake, and to a tenugui hand towel from Tenjinbashisuji in Osaka as a lightweight option.

### Which street suits a weekend trip from Tokyo?

JNTO suggests the shopping streets in front of Atami Station, where Heiwa-dori is a good street for snacking.

### Can I try before I buy?

JNTO says that at Ochanokosaisai in Nishiki Market you can sample everything before purchasing.
