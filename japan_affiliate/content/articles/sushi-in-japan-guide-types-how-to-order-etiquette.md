Sushi is the Japanese dish most visitors already know, and it is also the one they are most likely to get wrong. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes a sushi guide that clears up the common misunderstandings, explains the main styles and sets out how to behave at the counter. This page follows that guide, adds a practical way to choose where to eat, and labels our own opinion as "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## Four ways to eat sushi, and which one to pick

JNTO describes four kinds of place. Put side by side, they answer different needs.

| Where | What JNTO says | Our view: it suits |
|---|---|---|
| A neighborhood sushi bar | Per JNTO, {{fact:ssi_neighborhood}} | A first meal where you want a normal local place |
| A conveyor belt restaurant | Plates are chosen from the belt, and different colored plates have different prices | A relaxed meal, a group, or a family with children |
| A station standing bar | Many major train stations have small standing-room-only sushi bars | A quick bite between trains |
| An omakase counter | The chef has full control of the meal, and bookings are a must | A planned, special meal |

Our view: for a first visit, start with a conveyor belt restaurant or a neighborhood bar, and keep omakase for a trip when you have booked it weeks ahead. ## Common mistakes

- **Treating sushi as raw fish only.** JNTO says there is more to it, and it names vegetarian and cooked styles. Ask what is in a piece before you rule it out.
- **Dunking the rice.** The rice absorbs too much sauce and the piece can fall apart. Dip the topping.
- **Returning a plate to the belt.** JNTO says never to put a plate back.
- **Walking in on a popular counter.** JNTO says it is difficult on the same day, so book.
- **Treating the ginger as a topping.** It is a palate cleanser between pieces.

## FAQ

### Is sushi always raw fish?

No. JNTO says many people mistakenly believe sushi simply means raw fish, but sushi refers to vinegared rice paired with seafood, egg or vegetables, either raw or cooked.

### Should you use chopsticks or your hands for sushi?

Either. JNTO says chopsticks are the preferred utensil most of the time, but hands are perfectly acceptable, especially at higher-end restaurants.

### How do you use soy sauce with sushi?

JNTO says to dip the topping first and not the rice, because dipping the rice directly absorbs too much soy sauce.

### Do you need a reservation for sushi in Japan?

For a popular or omakase restaurant, yes. JNTO says bookings are a must for omakase and that walking into a popular restaurant on the same day tends to be difficult. A conveyor belt restaurant or a station bar does not need one in the same way.

### What is the difference between sushi and sashimi?

JNTO says sashimi is not sushi. It is raw fish served without rice.
