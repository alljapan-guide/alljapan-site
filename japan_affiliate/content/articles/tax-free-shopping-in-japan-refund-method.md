If you plan to shop in Japan, the tax-free rules have changed. From 1 November 2026 the tax-free system moved from an exemption at the register to a refund at departure. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has published a planning page and a detailed guide to the new system, and this page follows them. We add a practical order of operations for a visitor and label our own opinion as "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. The rules are set by the Japanese authorities, so treat this as a planning aid and confirm details on the official pages linked by JNTO before you buy.

## FAQ

### How does tax-free shopping work in Japan now?

From 1 November 2026, JNTO says travelers pay the full tax-inclusive price at tax-free shops. The consumption tax equivalent is refunded after Customs confirms the goods are being taken out of Japan.

### Where do I complete the procedure?

At a tax-free procedure terminal in the international departure lobby of your departure airport or seaport, before baggage check-in. At major international airports you can also begin online through Visit Japan Web.

### What does a green or red result mean?

JNTO says green means no customs inspection is required and the procedure is complete. Red means a customs inspection is required before the refund process can be completed.

### Can I ship tax-free goods home?

JNTO says that from 1 April 2025, tax-free shopping does not apply when the purchaser sends items overseas by international parcel. Direct shipping arranged by the store may still be available.

### Can I eat tax-free snacks before I leave?

JNTO says consumables are not eligible for a refund if they are consumed or used in Japan before departure.
