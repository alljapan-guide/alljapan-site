Tohoku is the northern part of Honshu, and it is the region where a short train ride from Tokyo takes you into a very different Japan: quieter towns, big landscapes and a festival season that draws crowds from across the country. It is easy to reach but hard to plan, because the best sights are spread along two long rail lines and a few long bus legs. This page is a planning hub. It does not repeat each guide; it helps you choose a starting point, compare the JNTO routes in one place and decide how many days to give the region. Every route comes from the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), and where we add opinion we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Tohoku?

JNTO's Tohoku routes run 2 to 5 days each. A first trip works well with the 3-day Sendai route, and the festival circuit needs 3 to 5 days.

### What is the easiest way to get to Tohoku?

JNTO says Sendai is best reached by the JR Tohoku Shinkansen, with trains up to seven times an hour. Trains on the same line run to Shin-Aomori hourly.

### Is Aizu a day trip from Tokyo?

JNTO describes Aizu as a 2-day itinerary and gives about 3 hours from Tokyo, so most visitors stay overnight.

### When is the best time to visit Tohoku?

It depends on your goal. The festival circuit is a summer plan, while the other routes can be done in other seasons; check the festival dates before you book.

### Do I need a car in Tohoku?

The JNTO routes in this guide use trains and buses. Some legs, such as the Aizu loop bus and the lake buses near Juniko, are infrequent, so check timetables first.
