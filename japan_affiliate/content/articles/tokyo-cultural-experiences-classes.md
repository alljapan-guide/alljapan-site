## How to build the day

Our view: choose two or three classes that sit close together on the route and give each a realistic window. Start with the sushi class, since it is a natural lunch, and then take the furin and origami classes in the afternoon, which keeps you on the central side of the city. Keep bonsai and calligraphy for a second day or a second visit if you want a slower pace. Between classes, leave time for the train, a short break and any delay at the studio.

## Questions to ask when you book

Our view: before you pay, ask four things. Is the class taught in English, or does it need a guide? How long does it last, including set-up and clean-up? What is the minimum age and the maximum group size? And what happens if you need to cancel? Small studios often have clear answers, and a short message before you book avoids surprises on the day.

## Who this day suits

Our view: this itinerary suits travellers who have done the main sights and want a more personal memory, as well as families and anyone who enjoys making things. It is a poor fit for visitors who dislike booking ahead or who want to move quickly between big landmarks. Pick the classes that match your interests, and leave gaps between them for travel.

## How to book and what to check

## FAQ

### How many days does the itinerary take?

JNTO's Cultural Experiences in Tokyo has a recommended length of 1 day.

### Are the classes suitable for beginners?

JNTO says the sushi class is suitable for all ages and beginners are welcome, and that the origami classes are open to all levels.

### What is a furin?

JNTO describes furin as small bulb-shaped glass wind chimes, which you can shape or paint at the workshop.

### Do I need to book the origami class?

JNTO says Origami Kaikan offers classes on an irregular basis, so check the schedule online before applying.

### Is there a calligraphy class?

JNTO lists Udoyoshi Calligraphy, where you can learn about the history and create your own pieces.
