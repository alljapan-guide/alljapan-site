Tokyo is a city of department stores, but some of the most interesting shopping happens in small stalls on old streets. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a one-day plan that links three of them: a street market near Ueno, a street of kitchen shops and the famous arcade leading to Sensoji. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "A Downtown Shopping Adventure in Tokyo" and adds practical notes. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## What to buy, and what to skip

Our view: choose items that you will actually use. Kitchen goods and food-themed gifts from Kappabashi are practical, and snacks from Nakamise are light to carry. Souvenirs sold in many stalls look similar, so compare two or three before you buy. Our [Japan souvenirs guide](japan-souvenirs-guide.html) and our [shopping street guide](shotengai-souvenir-shopping.html) explain what is worth carrying home.

## FAQ

### How long should you spend on the downtown shopping route?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 1 day, covering Ameyoko, Kappabashi and Nakamise.

### How do you get from Tokyo Station to Ameyoko?

JNTO says to take the JR Yamanote Line from Tokyo Station to Ueno Station for 10 minutes, then walk five minutes to Ameya Yokocho.

### What is Kappabashi known for?

JNTO says Kappabashi Street is also known as Kitchen Town, and knives, crockery and plastic food replicas are among its most popular souvenir items.

### What can you buy on Nakamise Street?

JNTO says it has 90-odd stalls selling kokeshi dolls, maneki neko figurines, Japan-themed t-shirts and kimono-type bathrobes, plus snacks such as red bean-filled sweets.

### When is the best time to see Sensoji?

JNTO suggests seeing Sensoji at night without the crowds, when it is beautifully illuminated.
