One week is enough for the classic route if you keep it simple: three nights in Tokyo, three in Kyoto with a day trip to Nara, and a final day in Osaka before you fly out. This plan favours fewer, better days over a long list of sights.

It assumes you arrive in Tokyo, travel west and depart from the Osaka area. If you fly in and out of the same city, adjust the last day. We give travel times only where an official source states them. Opening hours and tickets for individual sights change by season, so check each sight's official page before you go.

## How this itinerary is built

Seven days sounds like plenty, but moving between cities takes a half day once you add packing, getting to the station and checking in. To keep the trip relaxed we use two moves: Tokyo to Kyoto by bullet train, and Kyoto to Osaka by a short train ride. Everything else is a day trip from a base.

Two decisions help before you start. First, set up a transit card so you can tap through gates; see our [IC card guide](japan-ic-card-guide.html). Second, decide whether a rail pass makes sense. For a short route, run the sum in our [rail pass guide](is-japan-rail-pass-worth-it.html) before you buy.

{{tip|Book your Tokyo and Kyoto hotels first. They set the shape of the trip, and everything else can move around them.}}

## Day 1: Arrive in Tokyo

Aim for a gentle first day. After you land, travel to your hotel, drop your bags and take a short walk. Have an early dinner and sleep on local time. If you have not arranged mobile data, read our [eSIM vs pocket WiFi comparison](japan-esim-vs-pocket-wifi.html) and install a plan before you leave home.

Choose your base carefully, because you return to it every evening. Our guide to [where to stay in Tokyo](where-to-stay-in-tokyo.html) compares the main areas. You can compare hotels and cancellation terms on {{aff:booking|Booking.com}}, and Tokyo hotels are also listed on {{aff:trip_hotels|Trip.com}}.

## Day 2: Old and new Tokyo

Start in Asakusa. The official tourism guide describes Sensoji as {{fact:sensoji_oldest}}, and the temple is the heart of the area. Visit early if you prefer fewer people. Then cross the city to Shibuya for the famous crossing and an afternoon of browsing, and finish with dinner nearby. If you enjoy guided experiences, a food walk or evening tour on {{aff:klook|Klook}} or {{aff:trip_tokyo_things|Trip.com}} can add structure.

{{photo:3|Shibuya Crossing in Tokyo}}

{{note|Do not try to see everything. Pick two or three areas a day and leave gaps for wandering; that is our editorial advice for a first trip.}}

## Day 3: Harajuku, Meiji Shrine and Shinjuku

Spend the morning at Meiji Shrine, set in a large wooded park beside Harajuku. Walk on to the shopping streets of Harajuku, then take a train to Shinjuku for the afternoon and evening. The official guide says Shinjuku Station is {{fact:shinjuku_lines}}, which makes it easy to get back to almost anywhere.

## Day 4: Bullet train to Kyoto

Check out in the morning and take the Tokaido Shinkansen from Tokyo Station to Kyoto. JR Central gives the trip as {{fact:tokyo_kyoto_130}}. You can check fares and book on {{fact:jrc_booking}}. You can also look at Tokyo to Kyoto train options on {{aff:trip_trains|Trip.com}}, which is a third-party booking site. If you hold a pass, remember the pass rule: {{fact:nozomi_not_covered}}.

Drop your luggage at the hotel, or look into luggage forwarding so you do not carry bags through the station. Spend the afternoon in the Higashiyama area, then walk to Gion for dinner. If you want to hold a pass for the whole week, you can compare seller options through {{aff:railpass|an authorised Japan Rail Pass seller}}.

## Day 5: Fushimi Inari and central Kyoto

The Kyoto City Tourism Association gives the journey from Kyoto Station to the shrine as {{fact:fushimi_9min}}. Check the shrine's own official page for current hours and any rules, and go early if you prefer fewer crowds. You do not need to climb the whole mountain; the lower sections give you the classic views of the red gates.

{{photo:2|Deer in Nara Park}}

In the afternoon, choose between a temple visit and a slow walk through the old streets of central Kyoto. If you want a tea ceremony or a kimono rental, book ahead for the time you want.

## Day 6: Day trip to Nara

The official Nara travel guide gives the journey as {{fact:nara_jr_41}}; Kintetsu trains are an alternative, so check the operators' timetables. In Nara, walk through the park area (see the official {{fact:nara_park_guide}}) and visit Todai-ji. The deer are wild animals, so follow the park's official guidance on feeding and keep food out of reach.

If you prefer something quieter, spend the day around Arashiyama instead. Doing both in one day is possible but rushed.

{{warn|Opening hours, ticket rules and any festival closures are set by each temple or shrine. Check the official page for each one before you go.}}

## Day 7: Osaka and departure

Take a short train to Osaka. Leave your bags at your hotel or a station locker, then explore. Osaka is known for its food, so plan a meal rather than a checklist of sights. Allow a generous buffer for your trip to the airport, and check the official transfer information for your airport on your travel day.

## Choosing where to base yourself

Two bases are enough for this route:

- **Tokyo (nights 1 to 3):** see our hotel area guide for Shinjuku, Asakusa and the Tokyo Station area.
- **Kyoto (nights 4 to 6):** near Kyoto Station for transport, or in an older part of the city for atmosphere. This is our editorial view; check each area's access before you book.

Compare hotels with flexible cancellation, particularly for busy dates. Everything in this itinerary can be adjusted if a booking falls through.

## What to skip if you are short on time

- **Extra cities.** Resist adding more cities to a seven-day plan unless you drop something else.
- **Long queues for trending restaurants.** Choose neighbourhood places instead.
- **Overpacked days.** Two main activities a day is a healthy pace.

## FAQ

### Is 7 days enough for Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka?

Yes, if you accept that you will see highlights rather than everything. This plan uses two main moves to protect your time.

### Should I buy a Japan Rail Pass for this itinerary?

Do the sum in our rail pass guide first, using the official price and the fares for your dates.

### How do I get from Tokyo to Kyoto?

By the Tokaido Shinkansen from Tokyo Station. JR Central gives the journey as about 130 minutes.

### Where should I stay in Tokyo and Kyoto?

Our hotel guide explains the options for Tokyo. In Kyoto, choose between being close to the station or in an older area, and check access before you book.
