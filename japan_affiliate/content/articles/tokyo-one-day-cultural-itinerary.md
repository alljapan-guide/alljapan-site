If you have only one day in Tokyo and want history rather than neon, the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a route for it. It runs from the forest shrine at Meiji Jingu to the old neighbourhoods of Yanesen, with museums and parks in between. This guide follows the JNTO itinerary "A Cultural Day in Tokyo". Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate.

## How to shorten or stretch the day

Our view: the route has more than a day of sights, so decide your priorities before you leave. For a half day, keep Meiji Jingu and Ueno and stop there. For a slower pace, split it across two mornings, with the shrine on one and the park and the old neighbourhoods on the other. For rain, swap the outdoor stops for the museum and finish early. In every case, leave time for the evening walk through Yanesen, since JNTO describes it as the best place to spend the end of the day.

## Who this day suits

Our view: this itinerary suits travellers who like history, quiet streets and museums, and who are happy to walk between stops. It is a poor fit for someone whose priority is shopping, nightlife or modern attractions, and our [Tokyo things to do guide](tokyo-things-to-do-first-time.html) is a better starting point for that. If the weather is wet, move the garden stops later and keep the museums, since they are indoors.

## FAQ

### Is one day enough for Tokyo?

JNTO's cultural itinerary has a recommended length of 1 day, so it covers a focused theme rather than the whole city.

### How do I get to Meiji Jingu from Tokyo Station?

JNTO says to take the Yamanote Line to Harajuku Station, which takes 30 minutes, and walk one minute to the shrine entrance.

### What is in Ueno Park?

JNTO says it has museums, sacred sites and green surroundings, including the Tokyo National Museum and Shinobazunoike Pond.

### What is Yanesen?

JNTO describes it as a collection of three neighborhoods with market stalls, retro buildings and quaint eateries.

### Can I add another garden?

JNTO suggests Rikugien Garden, created in 1702, as an optional extra.
