The bullet train is the obvious way to get from Tokyo to Osaka, and it is simple once you understand one thing: Osaka has more than one station. This guide combines the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO), which describes the route, and JR Central, which runs the Tokaido Shinkansen and explains its booking system. Where we add opinion, we label it "Our view", so you can tell sourced information from editorial advice.

## A simple plan for the day

1. **Book in advance** if you want a specific time, using the online system or a platform.
2. **Arrive at the Tokyo station early** and check the platform on the display.
3. **Ride to Shin-Osaka**, about two and a half hours later, according to JNTO.
4. **Follow the JNTO transfer advice** to the JR Tokaido Line and then to Osaka Station.
5. **Drop bags at your hotel**, or send them ahead.

## FAQ

### How long does the bullet train from Tokyo to Osaka take?

JNTO says the trip takes approximately two and a half hours. JR Central says the fastest journey time was cut to 2 hours and 21 minutes in 2020.

### Which station do I arrive at?

The bullet train stops at Shin-Osaka Station. JNTO says to transfer to the JR local line and ride to Osaka Station.

### How far is Shin-Osaka from Osaka Station?

JNTO says the two stations are four kilometers apart.

### Can I book online from overseas?

JR Central says you can book both inside and outside of Japan, beginning one year before departure, and that a compatible credit card is required.

### Do I need to stop at a station counter before boarding?

JR Central says that with a QR-Ticket you display it and go directly to the ticket gate, with no need to stop at the counter.
