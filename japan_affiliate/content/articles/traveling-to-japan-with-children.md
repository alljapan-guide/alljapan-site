Parents planning a first trip to Japan with children usually worry about three things: whether the trains work with a stroller, whether the food will suit picky eaters and whether there is enough for kids to do. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) has a page of family travel tips, and the short answer it gives is reassuring. This guide summarises it and adds the planning details that make the trip smoother.

Where we add advice, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial opinion stay separate. Facilities differ by building and operator, so check the venues you plan to use.

## Is Japan child-friendly?

JNTO's answer starts from the big cities: they are surprisingly family-friendly, with smart design touches that help parents on the move. The page covers five areas, and the rest of this guide follows them: nursing rooms, trains, strollers, restaurants and things to do.

Our view: the main difficulty for families is not the destination, it is the logistics of moving children and bags between places. That is where the advice below helps most.

## FAQ

### Is Japan good for kids?

JNTO says the major cities are surprisingly family-friendly, with nursing rooms, stroller space on trains and welcoming restaurants.

### Can I take a stroller on Japanese trains?

Yes. JNTO says most urban trains have a space that can accommodate a stroller, and some carriages are marked as stroller-friendly.

### Can I rent a stroller in Japan?

JNTO says many sightseeing spots, including theme parks, museums and airports, offer free or low-cost rentals for families.

### Where can I find baby food?

JNTO says baby food is easy to find at most convenience stores.

### Are child car seats available?

JNTO says rental car companies often offer child seats as an optional extra, so request one when you book.
