## If you already have a bill

Our view: the steps below are common sense, not an official procedure.

- Ask the clinic what is owed and how to pay, and keep the receipt.
- Contact your insurer or card provider as soon as you can and follow its claim steps.
- If you are not sure whether you can pay before you leave, ask your insurer or the clinic what the options are.
- If you need language help, call the Japan Visitor Hotline. JNTO says that {{fact:emn_hotline}}.

{{warn|Do not ignore a bill because you plan to leave. JNTO's statement says that unpaid medical expenses may lead to entry being restricted or refused in future.}}

## Questions to ask yourself before you travel

Our view: a short self-check makes the risk concrete.

- Could I pay a large medical bill today, from savings or a card limit, if my insurer did not pay on the spot?
- Do I know whether my policy is cashless, or whether I would pay and claim later?
- Do I have the insurer's emergency number saved where I can find it in a hurry?
- Would I know how to find an English-speaking clinic, or whom to call for help?
- Am I travelling with someone who could help me with the practical steps if I were unwell?

If any answer is "no", fix that before you fly. The cost of preparation is small compared with the cost of a bill that is left unpaid.

## FAQ

### Can unpaid medical bills stop you entering Japan?

JNTO says that since 2021, foreigners who have unpaid medical expense in Japan may be restricted or refused entry to Japan.

### Is travel insurance mandatory for entry?

JNTO says it is strongly recommended for all travelers. It does not describe it as an entry requirement.

### How can I avoid an unpaid bill?

Get cover that pays for treatment, ask whether treatment is cashless, carry a payment method for a larger bill, settle any bill before you leave and keep receipts.

### Can I buy insurance after I arrive?

JNTO says it is possible to purchase insurance after you have arrived in Japan.

### Who can help me with language problems?

JNTO says the Japan Visitor Hotline supports English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese, and the AMDA International Medical Information Center offers advice and translation services.
