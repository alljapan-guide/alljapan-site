Getting ill on holiday is stressful anywhere, and in a country where you may not speak the language it can feel worse. The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) publishes practical guidance for visitors. This guide turns that guidance into a plan you can follow, so you know who to call and what to prepare. Where we give opinion, we label it "Our view", so sourced information and editorial advice stay separate. It is general information, not medical advice, and in an emergency you should call for help first and read afterwards.

## FAQ

### What number do I call for an ambulance in Japan?

JNTO says the number to dial for an ambulance is 119.

### Is there a help line for visitors who are ill?

JNTO says the Japan Visitor Hotline is open 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, with support in English, Chinese, Korean and Japanese for emergency support such as illness or disasters.

### What if I do not speak Japanese?

JNTO says some medical facilities offer services in foreign languages, but not all do, and the AMDA International Medical Information Center offers advice and translation services.

### Where do I find a clinic or hospital?

JNTO points to a Japan Tourism Agency guide for when you are feeling ill, available in English, Chinese and Korean.

### Will I need to pay upfront?

JNTO says some insurers may require you to pay upfront, and some clinics do not accept credit cards, so check your insurer's terms before you travel.
