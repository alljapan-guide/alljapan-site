Choose your Kyoto base by how you will move around, not by which neighbourhood looks prettiest in photos. Kyoto Station suits easy arrivals and departures, Shijo-Kawaramachi suits food and shopping, Gion and Higashiyama suit an old-Kyoto atmosphere, and the Gosho or Arashiyama areas suit a quieter stay.

This guide follows the Kyoto City Tourism Association's own description of each area, which we link in the Sources box. Where we give an opinion, we label it as our view, so you can tell sourced information from editorial advice.

## Kyoto Station area: simplest for arrivals

The official guide describes {{fact:kyoto_station_area}}. It also says the JR lines through this area give access to places such as Fushimi Inari Shrine, Tofuku-ji Temple and Arashiyama, and that there are many coin-operated lockers and luggage check-in places.

Our view: this is the safest choice for a short first trip, especially if you arrive or leave by bullet train. You trade atmosphere for convenience; the area around a big station is not the postcard Kyoto, so plan evenings in other districts. ## Gion and Higashiyama: the old-Kyoto feel

The official guide says that {{fact:kyoto_gion}}. The same page lists Kiyomizu-dera Temple, Yasaka-jinja Shrine and Kodai-ji Temple among the sights of this area.

Our view: if you want to wake up in a traditional setting, this is the area to look at first, and a ryokan stay can be the highlight of a trip. The trade-off is that you are further from the main station, so check the transfer route and your luggage plan before you book.

{{note|A ryokan is a traditional inn. Check what the booking includes, such as meals and check-in time, because the rules differ from a regular hotel. Read the property's own terms before you pay.}}

## Gosho and Nijo Castle: quieter and local

The official guide says that {{fact:kyoto_gosho}}. It lists Nijo Castle, Kyoto Gosho (the Imperial Palace) and Kitano-Tenmangu Shrine among the attractions, and mentions hotels and guest houses that blend with the mood of the local lifestyle.

Our view: choose this area if you value calm mornings and do not mind being a little away from the busiest evening districts.

## FAQ

### What is the best area to stay in Kyoto for first-timers?

Our view is the Kyoto Station area or Shijo-Kawaramachi. The official guide describes the Kyoto Station area as having many large hotels and business hotels, and Shijo-Kawaramachi as a commercial district with a wide range of lodging.

### Is it better to stay in Gion?

It suits travellers who want a distinctly Kyoto atmosphere, and the official guide recommends it for special occasions. Check the access to the station before you book.

### Do I need to take buses in Kyoto?

The official guide says buses can be very crowded depending on the season and recommends trains and subways. Choose a hotel with good rail or subway access.

### How long does it take to reach sights from the centre?

According to the official guide, one-way access to most locations in the central part is possible within an hour.

### Should I stay in Arashiyama?

Only if you prioritise scenery and quiet. The official guide notes that restaurants and cafes there tend to close early.
