For most first-time visitors, Shinjuku is the easiest Tokyo base because of the sheer number of rail lines that meet there. If you would rather stay in an older, calmer part of the city, Asakusa is the classic alternative. If your trip is built around bullet trains, the Tokyo Station area keeps early departures simple.

This guide separates what we can source from what is our own opinion. Facts such as which lines serve a station come from the official tourism guides, with links in the Sources box. Statements about atmosphere and who each area suits are our editorial view and are labelled that way.

## What to look for before you compare areas

Tokyo is huge, and no single neighbourhood is the city. These criteria matter more than any single hotel feature:

- **Station access.** A short walk to a major station saves more time than any amenity.
- **Your route in and out.** Think about how you will arrive from the airport and where you will go next.
- **Atmosphere.** Some areas stay busy late into the evening; others are calmer. Be honest about what you prefer.
- **Budget.** Decide a nightly budget first, then choose an area, since prices differ widely between areas and seasons.
- **Your itinerary.** If half your days are rail day trips, a hub station matters more than a scenic street.

## Shinjuku: the easiest all-round base

The JNTO guide to Shinjuku says Shinjuku Station is {{fact:shinjuku_lines}}. For a first visit that is the key point: whichever direction you want to go, you are likely to find a direct line.

Our view: this makes Shinjuku forgiving for first-timers, because you can change plans without changing hotels. The station is very large, so allow extra time to find the right exit on your first day, and pick a hotel within a few minutes' walk of a station exit.

{{tip|Check the walking route from the station to your hotel on a map before you book. With luggage, a short walk without many stairs matters more than a high star rating.}}

## Asakusa: traditional atmosphere

JNTO describes Sensoji as {{fact:sensoji_oldest}}, and the area around it is organised around that temple. Our view: this suits travellers who want an older feel to the city and a calmer evening than the big western hubs. Because the area sits to the east, check how many transfers you need to reach the places on your list.

## Tokyo Station and Marunouchi: the bullet train base

The official area guide for {{fact:tokyo_station_area}} covers this part of central Tokyo. Our view: staying near Tokyo Station is convenient if you leave early for another city by bullet train, because you avoid a transfer with luggage. Check the guide for what is nearby, and compare room prices, which can differ from the busier entertainment areas.

## Shibuya and other options

Shibuya is the area most visitors know for its famous crossing and shopping. We do not cite a source for any claim about it here, so treat the following as our editorial view only: it suits people who want a busy, youthful atmosphere and do not mind noise. Other areas such as Ueno or Ginza can also work well. Use the same checklist: station access, your route, atmosphere and budget.

## Choosing by trip style

| Your priority | Start with |
|---|---|
| First trip, maximum flexibility | Shinjuku |
| Older streets, quieter evenings | Asakusa |
| Early bullet train departures | Tokyo Station area |
| Busy atmosphere and shopping | Shibuya |

If you stay several nights, some travellers split their time between two areas for variety. That adds a hotel change, so weigh it against the extra packing and transfer.

## FAQ

### What is the best area to stay in Tokyo for first-timers?

Our editorial recommendation is Shinjuku, because the official guide shows it is served by many lines. Asakusa is a good alternative if you prefer an older, calmer area.

### Is it better to stay near Tokyo Station?

It is convenient if you have early bullet train departures. See the official area guide in the Sources box for what is nearby.

### Should I stay in two different areas?

For a longer stay it can add variety. For a shorter stay one base is simpler.

### Are Tokyo hotel rooms really small?

Room sizes vary. The listing gives the room size, so read it and check the photos before you book.

### When should I book?

As soon as your dates are fixed, especially for busy periods. Choose a flexible rate if your plans might change.
