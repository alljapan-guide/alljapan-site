## How to adapt the plan

Our view: if you only have one day, choose Masutomi Onsen as a day-trip destination and skip the temples. If you have a second night, stay in the Kofu area and add the orchards or wineries JNTO mentions. If you have a rental car, the temples and the mountain are much easier to link, so compare the cost of a car with the bus times before you decide.

## Who this trip suits

Our view: this trip suits travellers who want a quiet overnight with a bath, a regional meal and a small amount of walking. It is less suited to those who want a big sight or a lively town, since the villages are small and many shops close early. It works well in spring, when the temple gardens bloom, and in early summer, when the azaleas and irises on the mountain are at their best. Pack light, because you will carry your bag on buses and between stops, and bring a small towel for the bath. If you travel on a weekend, book your room first, since small hot spring inns have few rooms.

## FAQ

### How many days do you need for Yamanashi hot springs?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 2 days.

### How do you get from Tokyo to Enzan Station?

JNTO says it takes 1 hour 30 minutes on the JR Chuo Main Line Limited Express from Shinjuku Station.

### What is hoto?

JNTO says it is a warming hotpot in which flat, wheat-flour noodles are boiled with miso and seasonal vegetables.

### When is Mt. Amari at its best?

JNTO says to join the hikers in June to admire the Japanese azaleas and irises that cover the mountain.

### Can day-trippers use Hakusan Onsen?

JNTO says day-trippers are also welcome at Hakusan Onsen.
