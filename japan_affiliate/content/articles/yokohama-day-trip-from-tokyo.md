This guide follows the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) itinerary "Yokohama—From Past to Present". ## How to pace the day

Our view: start at the waterfront while the light is good for the skyline, and keep Landmark Tower for the late afternoon if the weather is clear, so you can see the city and then the lights. Plan the bus to Sankeien as the quiet middle of the day, and leave at least an hour there. If the weather is wet, move the museum and the Red Brick Warehouse indoors to the front of the plan, and keep the garden for another day.

JNTO's itinerary allows one to two days, so you can also split it: the waterfront and Chinatown on one day, and the garden and the museum on a second. That is a sensible choice if you are staying near Tokyo for several days and want a slower pace.

## Who this day suits

Our view: Yokohama suits visitors who want a relaxed day trip with a mix of waterfront, garden and food. It works for families because of Cosmo World and the cruise, and for couples because of the evening views. It is a weaker fit for travellers who want temples and old streets, in which case our [Kamakura guide](kamakura-day-trip-from-tokyo.html) is a better choice. If you are short on time, drop the museum or the garden and keep the waterfront and Chinatown.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to get from Tokyo to Yokohama?

JNTO says it takes 40 minutes, with frequent trains from different Tokyo stations.

### How many days do I need?

JNTO's itinerary has a recommended length of 1-2 days.

### What is Sankeien Garden?

JNTO describes it as a spacious Japanese-style garden that opened to the public in 1906, with historic buildings and teahouses.

### What is Yokohama Chinatown?

JNTO calls it Japan's biggest Chinatown, known as Chukagai, with restaurants, stores and food stands.

### Can I take a cruise?

JNTO says there are day and night cruises from the port, and an evening cruise shows the city in daylight and at dusk.
